Threat level
5 / 5 · Crisis
A second consecutive night without a CENTCOM strike announcement — confirmed by CNN and Iranian state media — marks the longest US military pause since the 13-night consecutive campaign began on 11 July, raising genuine ambiguity about whether a tactical hold is enabling diplomacy. Pakistan's Deputy PM Dar held calls with Saudi FM Prince Faisal and met China's Wang Yi at SCO-CFM in Kyrgyzstan on 25–26 July, with Beijing reiterating "firm support" for Islamabad's mediation; Iranian Interior Minister Momeni completed a second Islamabad visit in 10 days, meeting Army Chief Munir, signalling back-channel activity at high operational level. Against this diplomatic pulse, Houthis escalated kinetically: a strike on the Jizan Aramco refinery on 25 July and confirmed minor hull damage to a Saudi vessel in the Red Sea (Saudi Press Agency), while Saudi authorities issued simultaneous danger warnings for Yanbu and Jazan coastal zones. Brent opened 26 July at $100.67, traded $95–101, and sits structurally above $95 on dual-chokepoint supply deficit; the diplomatic pause in US strikes has not yet produced a Brent ceiling break below $90.

Six categories

01

Maritime / Hormuz

Crisis → Confidence: High
  • Kpler data (22 Jul, Reuters): 3 commodity vessels crossed Hormuz on Tuesday; zero VLCC or LNG transits visible — "effectively grinding to a halt" (Lloyd's List Intelligence).
  • CENTCOM states it has facilitated transit of ~900 commercial vessels and ~450 million barrels crude since early May under escort; but no large tankers broadcasting location in Hormuz corridor since 22 Jul.
  • IMO: ~20,000 seafarers impacted; evacuation plan currently paused; IMO Secretary-General condemned new attacks on ships in Hormuz (13 Jul press release).

Why it matters: Hormuz pre-crisis carried ~25% of world seaborne oil trade and ~20% of global LNG. At ~3 vessels/day (vs ~50 pre-crisis), the structural supply deficit is ~94% of former throughput. No VLCC transits means no meaningful crude volume moving; CENTCOM escort operates but does not restore commercial confidence.

Implication: Maintain Cape buffer at 60-day minimum. No sourcing from Gulf origin is viable without VLCC transit resumption. P(VLCC transit resuming without framework text) <5%.

Sources: Reuters / Kpler (Tier 2) 22 Jul; CENTCOM.mil (Tier 1) 20 Jul; Lloyd's List Intelligence (Tier 2) 10 Jul; IMO.org (Tier 1) 13 Jul.

Infrastructure / Energy

Crisis ↑ Confidence: High

  • Saudi Press Agency confirmed minor hull damage to Saudi vessel in Red Sea 25 Jul — crew safe, vessel continued to destination. War-risk premium ~0.75% vessel value for southern Red Sea (Seatrade Maritime Tier 2).
  • Brent: opened 26 Jul at $100.67, range $95.13–101.19; current ~$96.78 (Investing.com Tier 1). CPC Kazakhstan loadings remain suspended, ~80% of Kazakhstan crude exports disrupted (TradingEconomics Tier 1, confirmed 24 Jul).

Why it matters: The Houthi Jizan refinery strike represents a qualitative escalation — shifting from tanker harassment and commercial interdiction to kinetic attack on a fixed Saudi Aramco processing facility. If Yanbu export terminal is kinetically struck, Saudi Arabia loses its only functioning crude export corridor (Kpler: Yanbu handled 92% of Saudi seaborne exports in June 2026), removing the world's largest swing producer from effective participation in global supply for the duration.

Implication: P(Yanbu kinetic infrastructure strike by EOD 28 Jul) = 0.38 (↑8pp from Day 148; Jizan strike is Houthi calibration heuristic phase 2). Execute immediate Yanbu/Saudi-origin cargo review. Brent structural floor $95+ intact; diplomatic pause introduces $5–8 downside if framework signals strengthen.

Sources: CNN (Tier 1) 25 Jul; Saudi Press Agency (Tier 2) 25 Jul; Seatrade Maritime (Tier 2); TradingEconomics (Tier 1) 24 Jul; Kpler via AFP (Tier 2); TechTimes (Tier 3 — Jizan refinery strike; corroborated by CNN coastal warning signal).
05

Alternative Routes

Crisis → Confidence: High
  • Cape of Good Hope is the sole functioning large-scale alternative; Asian buyers "discussing rerouting Saudi crude shipments through the Suez Canal and around Africa" (TradingEconomics Tier 1, 24 Jul). Cape adds ~12–15 transit days and significant freight cost.
  • Yanbu / Saudi East-West pipeline: Houthi Jizan refinery strike 25 Jul + Saudi danger warnings for Yanbu 25 Jul confirm that the last bypass route is under active kinetic threat. Kpler: Yanbu handled ~92% of Saudi seaborne crude exports June 2026 — pipeline throughput irrelevant if port is denied.
  • Red Sea routing: Houthi enforcement active; multiple VLCC diversions; war-risk ~0.75% AV for southern Red Sea transits; tanker owners advised to scrutinise cargo origin, Yanbu port calls, and ownership (Seatrade Maritime Tier 2).

Why it matters: The architectural exploit the Houthis are pursuing: the East-West pipeline bypassed Hormuz but delivers to Yanbu, whose departing tankers must traverse Bab al-Mandeb — now under Houthi control. Closing Bab al-Mandeb kinetically or by deterrence neutralises the entire Saudi bypass architecture. The Jizan refinery strike is the first kinetic proof-of-concept against a fixed Aramco facility.

Implication: Cape buffer must be maintained at minimum 60 days. P(Yanbu fixed-infrastructure strike by EOD 28 Jul) now 0.38. If Yanbu port is kinetically struck, Cape becomes the only option and will saturate within 2–3 weeks for Asian-destination crude volumes.

Sources: TradingEconomics (Tier 1) 24 Jul; CNN (Tier 1) 25 Jul; Seatrade Maritime (Tier 2) 23 Jul; Kpler/AFP (Tier 2); TechTimes (Tier 3) 25 Jul.

  • Scenario C (Re-escalation): 80% (↓8pp). Still modal. Requires CENTCOM resumption or any of: Pickaxe strike, Yanbu kinetic, new Iranian GCC energy infrastructure strike, or Houthi Suez/northern Red Sea expansion.

Why it matters: The 8pp shift from Scenario C to B+A in one day is the largest single-session recalibration in this tracker's history, driven entirely by two CENTCOM no-announcement nights coinciding with the densest diplomatic contact cluster since the June MoU. This does not yet constitute a trend — it is a single-session signal. Anti-recency-bias check: the structural conditions (dual-chokepoint, CPC disruption, Jizan escalation, GCC ballistic attacks) have not changed. The pause could be operational (72h refit) rather than diplomatic.

Implication: Tonight's CENTCOM window (EOD 26 Jul ET = approximately 04:00 UTC 27 Jul) is the single highest-value observable gate for the next 12 hours. A third pause night would be the strongest signal to date that a diplomatic hold is in effect. Resumption collapses Scenario B back to ~8%.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
