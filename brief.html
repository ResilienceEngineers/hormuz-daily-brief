Six categories

01

Maritime Security

Severe → Confidence: High
  • Hormuz: ~14 transits/weekend (The National T2, 21 Sep) vs 125+/day pre-war; most AIS-dark; IRGCN northern corridor dominant; zero normal commercial tanker traffic.
  • Bab al-Mandeb: avg ~273 vessels/week post-blockade (Lloyd's List T2, 3 Sep); Houthi Mokha seizure (~10 Sep) caused one-day collapse to 6 transits; Saudi-linked tankers effectively excluded.
  • Lloyd's war-risk market: Saudi-linked vessels excluded from standard cover since 24 Jul; freight rates >$1M/day reported (Windward, 17 Sep); Chinese VLCCs appear to have secured Houthi exemptions (Windward T3).

Why it matters: Dual-chokepoint suppression is structurally simultaneous for the first time in the conflict — both Hormuz and BAM are functionally impaired, eliminating normal route arbitrage and forcing all non-exempted Gulf crude onto the Cape of Good Hope alternate. Each additional day extends the inventory drawdown clock for Asian and European crude consumers.

Supply-chain implication: Maintain Cape-routed tanker capacity bookings at premium; review October loading contracts for Yanbu force majeure exposure; do not assume BAM partial recovery will offset Hormuz loss — structural suppression at both chokepoints simultaneously = no viable route combination restores pre-crisis flow rates.

Sources: The National T2 (21 Sep), Lloyd's List Intelligence T2 (3 Sep), Windward T3 (17 Sep), Kpler via Tribune T3, UKMTO structural.
02

Military / Kinetic

Severe ↑ Confidence: High
  • PMF unconstrained window: US coalition formally exits Iraq 30 Sep (Soufan Center T3, today); Iraqi PM al-Zaidi delayed disarmament plan; Kataib Hezbollah and Nujaba refused compliance — kinetic constraint on PMF operations formally lifts from 1 Oct.
  • Houthi tempo sustained: Saudi coalition intercepted 2 ballistic missiles (Khamis Mushait) + 2 drones (Riyadh) on 26 Sep (Reuters T1); Houthis control Perim Island + Mokha on Red Sea coast; BAM overlapping fire corridors established.
  • France Yanbu deployment: Macron announced SAMP/T radars, air-defense, troops for Yanbu (24 Sep, TF1/France2 T1-T2); no troop numbers, equipment types, or arrival date given (MiGFlug T3, 25 Sep); deployment not yet operationally active — gap period is current vulnerability window.

Why it matters: The convergence of PMF unconstrained operations from 1 Oct, sustained Houthi kinetic activity against Saudi infrastructure, and a French deployment-logistics gap at Yanbu creates the highest kinetic risk window of the conflict to date. Kataib Hezbollah has stated publicly it will not disarm; historical PMF attack-initiation lag after US departure is typically 24–96 hours.

Supply-chain implication: Treat 1–7 October as the maximum kinetic risk period for Saudi energy infrastructure; do not schedule discretionary Yanbu loading windows during French AD gap; any Hard-confirmed PMF strike on Yanbu or Ras Tanura = immediate Threat-5 trigger and 72-hour supply-chain contingency activation.

Sources: Soufan Center T3 (28 Sep), Reuters T1 (26 Sep), MiGFlug T3 (25 Sep), militarnyi.com T3 (25 Sep), CNBC T2 (27 Sep).

Diplomatic

Elevated ↑ Confidence: Medium
  • Qatar shuttle active today (28 Sep): Qatari mediators holding separate meetings with Araghchi and Witkoff; Trump told Axios he expects talks "this week" (Axios T1, 27 Sep); first structured post-UNGA diplomatic contact.
  • Araghchi "doomsday" rhetoric (27 Sep, CNBC T2/NBC): FM stated Iran ready for "doomsday" war but leaving diplomacy on table; conditions unchanged — blockade-lifting, sanctions removal, ceasefire restoration; said intermediaries have "yet to relay a formal response from Trump."
  • IRGC hardliner constraint: Iranian hardliners demanded Araghchi/Pezeshkian impeachment for UNGA talks (RFE/Breitbart T3, 24 Sep); IRGC spokesman framed negotiations as "another facet of war" — domestic ceiling on Iranian flexibility is structurally low.

Why it matters: The Qatar shuttle represents the only active structural de-escalation mechanism in the conflict. However, the combination of Trump's public rejection of the 7-day plan, Araghchi's "doomsday" language, and IRGC hardliner domestic pressure means the conditions gap is wide and the Iranian political system has limited room to make concessions publicly. Sc A remains at 10% — alive but structurally constrained.

Supply-chain implication: Do not price Sc A recovery into procurement or hedging positions until a Hard joint statement with operational details (date, modality, sequencing) is publicly confirmed. Qatar shuttle activity is a Soft signal only — treat as optionality, not as de-escalation.

Sources: Axios T1 (27 Sep), TASS T3 (27 Sep via Axios), CNBC T2 (27 Sep), Breitbart/RFE T3 (24 Sep), Al Jazeera T2 (23 Sep).
04

Infrastructure

Elevated → Confidence: Medium
  • E-W pipeline ramp: Kpler estimates September Yanbu loadings ~2.9 Mb/d; Vortexa estimates ~3.7 Mb/d early September — both below pipeline's 7 Mb/d theoretical maximum; gap reflects Houthi BAM targeting deterrence on Saudi-linked vessels.
  • Khurais field: Repair of ~300,000 b/d capacity loss ongoing as of latest Argus/Energy Intelligence report (14 Sep); full restoration not yet announced — partial production impairment persists.
  • Yanbu infrastructure: Houthi claims of Aramco facility strikes 24 Sep (unverified per IRIA T3); large smoke plume at King Khalid International Airport Riyadh 19 Sep confirmed (Reuters/IRIA T3 via image credit) — sustained targeting of Saudi energy sites.

Why it matters: The East-West pipeline is Saudi Arabia's only functioning Hormuz bypass at meaningful scale; the Habshan–Fujairah UAE route adds ~1.5 Mb/d but is insufficient to offset full Gulf closure. Any repeat strike on a pumping station — particularly before French air-defense systems are operationally active — could reduce throughput by 700,000+ b/d within hours, as occurred after the 11 September attack.

Supply-chain implication: Model a 700,000 b/d throughput loss scenario at Yanbu as a 30-day planning contingency; identify alternative Red Sea loading windows at non-Saudi terminals (Djibouti, Suez anchorage); maintain 15-day crude buffer above operational minimum for Asia-Pacific and European refinery feedstock until French AD confirmed operational.

Sources: Tribune/AA T3 (Kpler, ~2 weeks ago), Vortexa via Tribune T3, Argus Media T2 (14 Sep), IRIA T3 (26 Sep), Reuters/IRIA image T3 (19 Sep).
05

Alternative Routes

Elevated → Confidence: Medium
  • Suez Canal recovery: Lloyd's List T2 (3 Sep): weekly Suez transits at highest since early 2024; Saudi crude rerouted northbound from Yanbu is driving additional volumes; bulk carriers, containerships, tankers all posting y/y gains.
  • Cape of Good Hope: Structural alternate for Saudi crude to Asia; voyage time uplift ~10–14 days vs Suez; bunker cost increase ~$500K–$1M per voyage; port congestion at Durban/Cape Town building as volumes increase.
  • UAE Fujairah bypass: Habshan–Fujairah pipeline operational at ~1.5 Mb/d; insufficient to absorb Hormuz volume loss alone but provides partial UAE crude egress; Chinese VLCCs transiting BAM with apparent Houthi exemptions (Windward T3, 17 Sep) suggest targeted commercial workaround for non-Saudi cargoes.

Why it matters: No single alt-route combination restores pre-crisis flow volumes. The Suez recovery is real but driven by northbound rerouting of Yanbu exports — itself at risk from Houthi re-attack. If the E-W pipeline is re-struck, the Suez recovery reverses simultaneously. Alt-route resilience is serially dependent on Yanbu throughput holding.

Supply-chain implication: Do not treat Suez recovery as structural normalization — it is downstream of Yanbu export volume, which is itself at risk. Maintain Cape routing capability as a non-Yanbu-dependent backup; Chinese-exemption VLCC route is not available to non-Chinese operators and cannot be replicated at scale.

Sources: Lloyd's List Intelligence T2 (3 Sep), Windward T3 (17 Sep), Kpler via Tribune T3, RedState T3 (25 Sep).

Strategic Outlook

Severe ↑ Confidence: High
  • PMF kinetic risk window: 1 Oct onward is the first period with no US-troop constraint on PMF cross-border operations; Kataib HB stated publicly it will not disarm; historical precedent (2011, 2021) shows kinetic initiation within 24–96h of US departure.
  • Post-midterm bombing signal (structural): WSJ T1 reported Trump told aides he expects to resume bombing after November midterms; Araghchi "doomsday" counter-signal (27 Sep) suggests Tehran has internalized this assessment and is signaling resolve.
  • Signal against trend: Qatar shuttle is active today with both sides meeting mediators separately (Axios T1) — this is the most structurally significant de-escalation signal since the June MoU collapsed; Trump's "expects talks this week" is a Soft upward signal for Sc A.

Why it matters: The 30-day outlook sits at 44% re-escalation / 46% frozen attrition / 10% negotiated framework — a distribution where the modal scenario (Sc B) and the tail scenario (Sc C) are nearly equally weighted. The PMF kinetic window opening 1 Oct is the most proximate Hard trigger for a Sc C step-change. The Qatar shuttle is the only structural counter-signal; its failure to produce a framework by ~5 Oct would push Sc C probability toward 50%.

Supply-chain implication: Maintain dual-scenario planning posture: Sc B (frozen attrition) = sustain alt-route infrastructure and 15-day buffer stocks; Sc C (re-escalation) = pre-position for Brent spike toward $115, activate force majeure review on all Gulf-origin contracts, and shift to Cape-only routing for the next 30 days. Do not reduce buffer stocks before French Yanbu AD is confirmed operationally active.

Sources: Soufan Center T3 (28 Sep), Axios T1 (27 Sep), CNBC T2 (27 Sep), WSJ T1 (prior week), TradingEconomics T2 (28 Sep).

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
