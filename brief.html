Maritime Security

Severe ↑ Confidence: High

  • UKMTO Advisories 197-26 and 198-26 remain active; Omani Royal Navy rescued crew from Indian-crewed dhow (UKMTO 8 Nov). Vessel transits estimated ~8/day vs 130+ pre-war. 13th consecutive day with Hard-tier vessel incident signal.

Why it matters: The dual-layer harassment (IRGC mines + navigation-aid destruction) compounds transit risk beyond what any single metric captures. CENTCOM's denial of mine causality on the primary route does not resolve the UKMTO-corroborated explosion events — the operational reality is that vessels face kinetic risk on both declared and undeclared routes.

Supply-chain implication: Tanker operators must choose between CENTCOM-escorted primary route (kinetic risk disputed but present) or non-escorted routes (IRGC mine claims credible). War-risk insurance exits accelerating. Cape rerouting extends transit by 2–3 weeks and raises per-cargo cost by $1.5–2.5M. Inventory buffer consumption continues; European refined-product holders should model 30–45 day buffer depletion curve.

Alternative Routes

Degraded → Confidence: High
  • Bab al-Mandeb structurally contested: Houthis hold Perim/Mayyun (seized inside the strait, Sep 2026), Hanish islands (Sep 2026), and Dhubab/Mokha coastal position — controlling both sides of the strait's southern approach. Houthis manage Red Sea transit permissions via selective authorization protocol. No free transit available without Houthi approval.
  • Saudi Multinational Maritime Security Alliance active since August 2026 (Carnegie/Africa Center). US officials met Houthi representatives in Muscat Sep 2026 — Houthis assured US shipping not targeted; restrictions apply to Saudi-linked vessels. Assurance credibility disputed. US military capacity to open a Yemen front assessed as constrained (AJ Sep 2026 — US resources depleted, domestic opposition).
  • Aviation: Singapore Airlines SQ494/SQ495 suspended through February 2027; Cathay Pacific Dubai suspension through January 2027 (Gulf News 9 Nov). Cape of Good Hope route: operational but adds 2–3 weeks transit time and $1.5–2.5M per cargo cost. No viable fully-open alternative maritime route to Hormuz at scale.

Why it matters: Both primary alternative routes to Hormuz (Bab al-Mandeb/Suez and Cape of Good Hope) are either structurally contested or prohibitively expensive. The dual-chokepoint stress — Hormuz blocked by IRGC/CENTCOM confrontation and BaM controlled by Houthis — is unprecedented in the modern energy era and eliminates the standard supply-chain mitigation playbook.

Supply-chain implication: Cape rerouting is currently the only operationally viable alternative but is approaching saturation for time-sensitive refined products. Cargo with hard delivery deadlines must book Cape routing immediately — do not wait for a Hormuz resolution that may not arrive before the Roosevelt window opens. BaM-dependent supply chains (Europe-Asia) face compounded risk if Houthi authorization is withdrawn.

Sources: Al Jazeera (Tier 2), Carnegie Endowment (Tier 3), Africa Center (Tier 3), Gulf News (Tier 2), Euronews (Tier 2), OSINT613 (Tier 3)

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
