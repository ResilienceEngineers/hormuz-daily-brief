Maritime Security

Crisis → Confidence: High
  • Hormuz: 3 transits Saturday 25 Jul, 7 Sunday 26 Jul (Kpler/Japan Times Tier 1) — bilateral pause has not restored commercial traffic; US naval blockade explicitly maintained (CENTCOM 26 Jul Tier 1)
  • Kharg Island: 24 vessels in waiting area 26 Jul; all 3 loading terminals (Eastern, LPG, Western) cleared — Windward MIOC assesses steady throughput, not disruption (Windward MIOC Tier 2, 27 Jul)
  • Red Sea: tanker struck 26 Jul with minor hull damage, continued to destination; vessel had gone AIS-dark leaving Yanbu (Windward MIOC Tier 2); Houthi Saudi embargo remains kinetically enforced

Why it matters: The bilateral military pause has produced zero observable improvement in Hormuz commercial transit volumes. Iran's leverage is its control of the strait; the US naval blockade and IRGC warnings remain structurally intact. A diplomatic deal that does not explicitly address the transit regime cannot move vessel numbers.

Implication: Do not interpret the operational pause as a transit recovery signal. Hormuz remains effectively closed to commercial shipping. AIS-dark sailing remains the only transiting mode for non-Iranian vessels, carrying severe insurance and crew liability exposure. Cape routing should be treated as the default for at least 30 more days regardless of summit outcome.

Sources: Japan Times/Kpler (Tier 1) 27 Jul; USNI News/Lloyd's List (Tier 1) 24 Jul; Windward MIOC (Tier 2) 27 Jul; CENTCOM.mil (Tier 1) 26 Jul.

Alternative Routes

Crisis ↓ Confidence: High
  • Bab al-Mandeb: 7 vessels Sunday 26 Jul including 7 oil tankers; 11 cargo vessels Friday 25 Jul (Kpler/Hill Tier 1) — still critically below pre-crisis ~250/day; Houthi Saudi embargo remains active with "escalation with escalation" vow
  • Cape of Good Hope: Asian buyers — including Hong Kong VLCC New Explorer carrying 2 mb Saudi/Emirati crude to Ningbo — routing via Cape (Japan Times/Kpler Tier 1); Saudi crude also reaching Pakistan via Cape (Japan Times Tier 1)
  • Yanbu: 2 VLCCs heading toward port to load despite Houthi missile threat (Japan Times Tier 1) — risk appetite returning but Houthi declared embargo creates sustained interdiction risk on loading and departure

Why it matters: Bab al-Mandeb is architecturally separate from any US–Iran deal. A Hormuz transit compromise does not end Houthi enforcement on Saudi Red Sea exports. The Cape route is the only corridor currently functioning reliably, but it cannot absorb the full volume of disrupted Gulf flows at scale — voyage time and insurance cost differentials are structural supply chain constraints.

Implication: Extend Cape buffer to 90-day minimum. Do not rely on Bab al-Mandeb normalisation within a 30-day planning horizon regardless of US–Iran framework outcome. P(Yanbu sustained physical damage EOD 30 Jul) = 0.48 — if triggered, Cape-only becomes the sole mode for Saudi crude, fully saturating available VLCC capacity.

Sources: Japan Times/Kpler (Tier 1) 27 Jul; The Hill/Kpler (Tier 1) 28 Jul; CNBC (Tier 1) 26 Jul; USNI News (Tier 1) 24 Jul.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
