Day 203 of the US/Israel–Iran war. Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline (offline since 10 Sep) remains shut with bypass construction underway; Bloomberg (Hard T1, 16 Sep) reported half-capacity restart targeted "within days," but as of 06:00 UTC 18 Sep no Hard confirmation of actual flow restored has emerged. Yanbu Aramco terminal damage from the 15 Sep satellite-detected fires (NASA FIRMS / Energy News Beat, T3) remains unconfirmed by Aramco or Saudi Energy Ministry — Kpler (T1, 17 Sep) confirms zero crude loadings since 11 Sep but attributes the stoppage to pipeline shutdown, not a separate Yanbu terminal strike. Brent traded at ~$103.72 on 18 Sep, retreating from $109 peak as bypass news provided partial relief; the triple-pathway risk (Hormuz structurally closed + East-West offline + Yanbu loading suspended) remains the modal structural threat. Houthi forces retain de facto BAM control — Perim, Hanish, and full Red Sea coastline held — with no formal closure declaration issued but operational chokepoint conditions met.

Implication: VLCC war-risk premium at $30–32/bbl (OilPrice 15 Sep) is now structural, not episodic. Cargo insurers are pricing permanence. Supply chain posture: dark-transit volumes are the marginal barrel — monitor Kpler AIS daily for deviation from ~10/day floor.

Implication: Brent $100–110 structural band. Bypass activation = −$3–5 short-term relief; Yanbu Hard confirmation = $110+ floor; triple-pathway Hard = Goldman $120 tail. Alternative Routes

Crisis ↑ Confidence: High
  • Houthi BAM de facto control: full Yemen Red Sea coastline + Perim Island + Hanish Islands held mid-Sep 2026 (Wikipedia BAM crisis, T3; AJ T1, 13 Sep); positioned 20km from African coast; no formal closure declaration but operational chokepoint conditions met
  • Saudi Red Sea exports: 99% Saudi-flagged vessels in Saudi territorial waters (Windward MIOC T3, 11 Sep); Yanbu loadings zero; Aramco pivoting ~20 mb prompt sales to Asian refiners via Hormuz dark transits (Bloomberg Hard T1, 16 Sep)
  • Cape routing: dominant; +14–18 days vs Suez baseline; Suez northbound Saudi crude near-zero; European cargoes cancelled through November; Djibouti congestion signal (2,000+ refugees 13 Sep; IOM)

Why it matters: Both primary export corridors (Hormuz and Red Sea/Yanbu) are simultaneously impaired. The East-West pipeline was the inter-connector between them. Its outage means even a partial Hormuz reopening cannot easily be supplemented by Yanbu. With Cape routing dominant but congestion building, the resilience of the alt-route system itself is beginning to erode.

Implication: Cape unconditional for all Saudi crude through at least end-October. Monitor Cape port congestion at Durban, Cape Town, and Saldanha Bay as bellwether for alt-route saturation. Yanbu bypass restart = reopens Red Sea leg; Houthi formal BAM closure = FM trigger for all Saudi Red Sea cargo regardless of pipeline status.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
