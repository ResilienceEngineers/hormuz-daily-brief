Maritime Security & Chokepoints

Severe ↑ Confidence: High

IRGC 11 Nov strike on tanker engine room 14nm NW Ras al-Khaimah (crew evacuated) is an escalation in target precision compared to prior-week incidents. Weekly transit volume fell further (308 vs 325 prior week) despite US escort operations.

Supply chain implication: Structural rerouting locked in; war-risk insurance premiums remain elevated; Hormuz transits unlikely to recover absent Hard-confirmed diplomatic agreement. Any UKMTO advisory citing mine detonation on US-escorted vessel remains Threat-5 candidate. Yanbu damage confirmation would remove Saudi's Red Sea bypass buffer simultaneously.

Sustained Houthi targeting of this route is a campaign pattern, not isolated events (also struck 10 Nov Riyadh Province).
  • Brent crude: $95.72 close 13 Nov (Dealplexus/EIA); −9.6% over 30 days; recovering from $91.5 low 10 Nov; EIA Nov 2026 STEO forecasts avg ~$100/b for remainder of year; MacroRadar/DCOILBRENTEU Nov avg ~$107/b (3 Nov data point). Alternative Routes & Resilience

Stressed ↑ Confidence: Medium
  • Saudi East-West pipeline (Petroline): primary Hormuz bypass for ~4 MMbpd now under sustained Houthi attack campaign (9 Nov satellite-confirmed; 12 Nov claim unconfirmed); prior Sep 2026 shutdown demonstrated pipeline vulnerability to multi-station attacks. Saudi Aramco repair capability confirmed but cumulative stress increasing.
  • Bab al-Mandeb: Lloyd's List (12 Nov) — 1,363 transits Oct (up 17% MoM, up 63% YoY); approximately half of all containership transits "dark" (AIS disabled), signalling persistent operator risk aversion. No Houthi attacks since 24 Aug 2026 but Houthis now emailing transit permits to non-US/UK/Israeli vessels (WSJ, 6 Nov) — de facto toll system emerging. Houthi infra pivot to Saudi territory ongoing.
  • UAE Fujairah bypass: Habshan-Fujairah pipeline operational; structural alt-route for UAE/Abu Dhabi crude. Saudi East-West degradation increases reliance on UAE bypass for Gulf producers. Cape of Good Hope routing: dominant for major container carriers (Maersk, Hapag-Lloyd, MSC); CMA CGM limited Red Sea return with naval escort; +10–14 days + 25–30% cost premium vs Suez persists.

Why it moved: The 12 Nov Houthi East-West/Yanbu strike campaign directly targets Saudi Arabia's alt-route buffer — the pipeline that has been the primary Hormuz bypass since the Iran blockade began. The simultaneous pattern of Houthi infra targeting and Hormuz IRGC maritime campaign means both primary routes are now under concurrent attack. UAE Fujairah alone cannot compensate for Petroline disruption at scale.

Supply chain implication: Procurement teams sourcing Middle East crude should model scenarios where Yanbu is unavailable for 7–21 days following confirmed pipeline shutdown; identify alternative origin flexibility (US Gulf, West Africa, North Sea) and spot market access. Confirm insurance coverage includes dual-chokepoint disruption language. Cape routing cost premium likely persistent through Q1 2027.

Sources: Lloyd's List Intelligence Red Sea Brief (Tier 2, 12 Nov); P&GJ/Reuters pipeline history (Tier 2, 10 Nov); WSJ Houthi permit system (Tier 2, 6 Nov); East-West Pipeline Wikipedia with sourced Sep 2026 shutdown references; GoFreight/Kühne+Nagel Cape routing data (Tier 3).

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
