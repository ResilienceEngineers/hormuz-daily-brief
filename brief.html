Threat level
5 / 5 · Crisis
The operational pause that had suppressed kinetic activity since 24 July collapsed on 28 July when the IRGC fired multiple ballistic missiles at US forces in Jordan at 5:45 pm ET — all intercepted by CENTCOM, which then conducted joint precision strikes with Saudi Arabia against IRGC-directed militia logistics across eastern Iraq. Iran simultaneously rejected Oman's 50-50 Hormuz control proposal, demanding Iranian-majority oversight of both shipping lanes — a structurally blocking position that moves the probability of a near-term Hormuz reopening to near zero. The Trump–Netanyahu White House summit produced no framework text, no joint de-escalation signal, and confirmed Iran's public denial of direct US negotiations. Dual-chokepoint lock-in is now the operative scenario: Hormuz at ~6 vessels/day, Bab al-Mandeb at ~28 vessels/day (vs. 250+ pre-crisis), Jazan refinery shut at 400k b/d, and Houthi drones targeting the East-West pipeline link to Yanbu — the last Saudi seaborne crude corridor.

01

Maritime / Hormuz

Crisis ↑ Confidence: High
  • Kpler / Reuters (Tier 1, 27–28 Jul): Hormuz at 6 commodity vessels on 27 Jul and 7 on 26 Jul — structurally closed; one Iranian-linked Suezmax and one AIS-dark VLCC among transits
  • CENTCOM (Hard Tier 1, 28 Jul): "As of July 28, CENTCOM has redirected 18 commercial vessels, disabled 2, and boarded 2 to ensure full compliance" with blockade; 20+ US Navy warships operating across Middle East
  • Iran Int'l / WSJ (Tier 1–2, 28 Jul): Iran rejects Oman 50-50 Hormuz control proposal; Iranian DFM Gharibabadi insists one full lane and part of second must lie within Iranian territorial waters — structurally blocking position

Why it matters: Iran's counter-proposal to Oman's mediation framework reveals that Tehran's minimum acceptable Hormuz position (sovereign Iranian-majority control of both lanes) is categorically incompatible with the US position (international waterway, free transit). The June MOU's collapse on exactly this point is now being repeated. With CENTCOM blockade enforcement tightening and Iran's surprise Jordan strike ending the pause, the probability of a Hormuz reopening protocol within 30 days is near zero absent a major capitulation by either party.

Implication: Extend planning horizon for Hormuz closure to 60+ days. All Gulf-origin crude contracts exposed. Cape routing is the only commercially viable corridor for non-Iranian tankers; at current VLCC saturation pace (~43 AIS-gap events in 7 days per Windward), vessel availability will constrain physical supply within 30–45 days.

Sources: Kpler/Reuters (Tier 1) 27–28 Jul; CENTCOM (Hard Tier 1) 28 Jul; Iran International (Tier 1) 28 Jul; WSJ (Tier 1) 28 Jul; Windward MIOC (Tier 2) 28 Jul; Crisis Group Hormuz tracker (Tier 1) Jul.

Why it matters: Saudi Arabia's seaborne crude export system is now under coordinated kinetic attack on three simultaneous nodes: (1) Hormuz closed — Gulf corridor gone; (2) Jizan refinery shut — Red Sea refined product output reduced by 400k b/d; (3) East-West pipeline network targeted — overland link between eastern fields and Yanbu threatened. If the Abqaiq pipeline strike is confirmed to reduce throughput, Saudi Arabia loses the last reliably functioning crude export corridor. The Brent price collapse on diplomacy hopes was predicated on the pause holding — that assumption is now invalidated by the IRGC Jordan strike, and structural floor should reassert toward $90–95.

Implication: Brent structural floor: $88–95 under pause; $98–105 if Abqaiq pipeline damage confirmed. FM clause review on all Saudi-origin and Iraq-origin contracts is unambiguously triggered. Jazan refinery shutdown is a confirmed physical damage FM event for refined products contracts with Saudi/Red Sea origin provisions.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
