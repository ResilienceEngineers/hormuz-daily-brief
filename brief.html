On Day 163, Iran's Supreme National Security Council issued sweeping new conditions for Strait of Hormuz reopening — demanding US naval blockade lift, sanctions removal, troop withdrawal, war reparations and frozen asset release — hours after the IRGC struck an ADNOC tanker transiting the strait, the 16th vessel hit since conflict began. Iran's FM Araghchi confirmed a deal with Oman is "very close" but explicitly stated it will not by itself reopen the waterway. Iran's parliament is simultaneously reviewing a draft Hormuz bill barring US and Israeli vessels and imposing 20% cargo-value penalties. The Iran-Oman joint statement has now missed five consecutive deadlines; the Deadline-Slip Heuristic is upgraded to maximum-discount status. Yanbu terminal continues operating at high load but faces elevated Houthi threat following the NCC Wafa strike (5 Aug); no new Yanbu infrastructure hit confirmed as of 06:00 UTC.

Deadline-Slip Heuristic now at maximum discount — five consecutive misses from "finalized"/"very close" language without Oman FM independent confirmation.
  • Iran SNSC maximalist demands (Reuters T1 / CNBC T1, 8 Aug): Iran's Supreme National Security Council stated strait won't reopen until US "corrects its behavior" — specifically: lift naval blockade, remove sanctions, withdraw military forces from region, pay war reparations, release frozen Iranian assets.   • Iran parliament draft Hormuz bill (Reuters T1 / Fars T2, 6 Aug): Parliamentary National Security and Foreign Policy Committee reviewing bill: bans US/Israeli vessels, imposes 20% cargo-value penalties on violators, requires hostile-nation compensation before transit. Still under expert review — not yet enacted. Simultaneously, Iran state media reported lawmakers awaiting "final approval" on Oman framework. Dual-track signaling: SNSC maximalism + parliament bill + FM "close" language = fractured internal signaling.

Why it matters: The Iran SNSC statement on 8 Aug is the most significant negative diplomatic signal since the MoU collapse. It explicitly decouples the Iran-Oman technical deal from the Hormuz reopening — meaning even a signed joint statement would not reopen the strait. This collapses the market's implicit assumption that a deal text = reopening. The parliament bill, if enacted, legally codifies the blockade and structurally forecloses Scenario A.

Implication: Scenario A downgraded to 32% (−4pp). Do not trade on "deal close" language until: (1) Oman FM confirms signed text; (2) SNSC conditions are publicly withdrawn or modified; (3) AIS shows ≥3× transit uplift. The gap between FM technical deal and SNSC political conditions is now the primary diplomatic risk variable. Scenario B (frozen attrition) elevated to 47% as most likely 30-day path.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
