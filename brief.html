Scenario B (frozen attrition) remains modal at 43%; Scenario A edges to 30% on sustained mediator pressure but cannot cross 35% without SNSC action and a US acceptance signal.

Maritime Security

Severe → Confidence: High (Hard T1 sourced)
  • No new UKMTO-confirmed strikes 28–29 Aug — 4-day lull since Khasab (25 Aug); longest pause in 2 weeks but below structural reassessment threshold.
  • US CENTCOM: "over the past several months, US forces have assisted nearly 1,500 commercial vessels in transiting the strait" — ongoing coordinated protection required; voluntary transit not viable.
  • UKMTO 087 (Red Sea/BAM) in force; MV Seamull + 5 vessels held; Houthi selective enforcement targeting Saudi-linked assets; collateral risk elevated for non-targeted commercial shipping.

Why it matters: The 4-day IRGC kinetic lull (24–29 Aug) is the longest since the Khasab/Ash Shishah cluster, but coincides with the diplomatic surge — consistent with the confirmed heuristic that IRGC operates on a separate command track and resumes spoiling when diplomatic momentum peaks. The lull is not a structural indicator; the baseline threat posture (UKMTO 087, blockade, no-escort-no-transit) remains unchanged.

Implication: War-risk exclusions remain absolute. OFAC PGSA fee guidance in force. Cape routing mandatory. Do not treat lull as signal of IRGC stand-down; treat as pre-resumption posture consistent with spoiling pattern.

Sources: UKMTO Attack 121-26 (Hard T1) 25 Aug; UKMTO 087 (Hard T1) in force; MARAD-006 (Hard T1) in force; CBS News (T1) 28 Aug — CENTCOM 1,500-vessel quote; Windward AI (T2) 27 Aug.

Diplomatic

Elevated ↑ Confidence: High (T1 multi-source corroborated)

Why it matters: Araghchi's "not impossible" language is the strongest public diplomatic signal from Iran since the war began, but it is an FM-level Soft signal conditioned on US abandoning pressure — structurally incompatible with Bessent's "coercive sequencing" posture and Trump's "cure period" framing. The gap between FM rhetoric and SNSC/Khamenei-office action remains the defining diplomatic constraint. Mojtaba Khamenei's continued absence from public view (Iran Intl, June) means authority is dispersed and written-message attributed — adding uncertainty to SNSC response timing.

Implication: Sc A = 30% maintained (not upgraded — SNSC gate missed). US direct channel closure = structural cap on Sc A at current level. Sc B (frozen attrition) = 43% modal. Next gate: SNSC endorsement by EOD 31 Aug; absence = Sc A decays to ~24%.

Implication: Cape routing unconditional. No Hormuz or BAM corridor transit without US military escort and explicit OFAC clearance. P(Yanbu impairment within 7 days) = 0.08 (maintained). P(Cape re-entry conditions met within 14 days) = 0.06.

  • Sc B (Frozen attrition / diplomatic limbo): 43% — maintained as modal. Bessent "cure period" + Araghchi "pressure doesn't work" are mirror-image blocking postures; both sides are managing escalation not resolving it. Six-month structural assessment supports B as durable baseline.
  • Sc C (Re-escalation): 27% — maintained. IRGC 4-day kinetic lull is insufficient to reduce — structural spoiling pattern confirmed; major-bank designation (if Chinese) = Sc C upside trigger; Rezaei "active deterrence" rhetoric = preparation framing.

Why it matters: Six-month milestone without resolution is itself a structural signal — the Sc B frozen-attrition mode is now self-reinforcing on both sides. Iran's economic deterioration is real (Chinese intake reduced from 1.4 mb/d to ~534,000 bpd) but IRGC institutional incentive to maintain strait leverage does not change. Signal against current trend: the Araghchi "not impossible" language, combined with the revenue-sharing framework announcement, represents the most substantive joint diplomatic signal since the war began — if SNSC endorses by 31 Aug, Sc A would upgrade to 35%+.

Implication: Supply-chain posture: maintain full Cape routing, elevated buffer stocks on refined products, and hedged long-tenor contracts. The Bessent major-bank designation (gate today, EOD ET) is the single most operationally relevant trigger for the next 24 hours. Monitor and pre-position FM review protocol.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
