Day 160 of the US/Israel–Iran war: the outlines of a 60-day interim Hormuz shipping arrangement have entered the public domain via Axios and Bloomberg — inbound via a northern Iranian-waters lane, outbound via a southern Omani-waters lane, no tolls, mine clearance within 30 days — but Tehran and Muscat have not released a signed joint statement, and the IRGC's institutional veto remains structurally live. Aramco's Q2 2026 results cited "continued utilization of the East-West Pipeline" without any Abqaiq-specific throughput declaration, extending the information hazard into Day 12; CENTCOM interceptor stocks are now assessed at ~80% THAAD depletion and ~50% Patriot depletion per CNN/GlobalSecurity, constraining resumption calculus.

Maritime Security

Crisis → Conf: High

  • MSV Faize Noore Oliya sunk (Hard T1 — UKMTO/FreightWaves, 4 Aug): Indian-flagged mechanized vessel struck by explosive-laden boat ~13nm S of Hodeidah; sank; 14 crew rescued by Yemeni Coast Guard. Fourth Houthi attack since 1 August per FreightWaves.
  • Kpler w/e 2 Aug (T1): 106 Hormuz crossings by 96 vessels (+8% w/w); 41% AIS-dark; 6.4% using IMO lane since March (Kpler, 26 Jun). Bab al-Mandeb: 11 cargo vessels 26 Jul — near-record low.

Why it matters: Both primary chokepoints remain effectively closed to non-approved commercial traffic. The slight Hormuz transit uptick (+8% w/w) is deal-anticipation noise — volatile daily figures (8–25 crossings), 41% dark-AIS, and UKMTO strike warnings preclude operational normalization. The Houthi sinking of MSV Faize Noore Oliya is the most severe Red Sea kinetic event since the Saudi blockade declaration (20 Jul), signalling escalating willingness to destroy rather than merely deter vessels.

Implication: Do not reduce war-risk insurance posture on deal-text signals alone. AIS uplift ≥3× sustained ≥72h + insurer re-entry = minimum observable before routing switch. For Bab al-Mandeb, nationality-discriminatory pattern (Chinese vessels unimpeded, Saudi-linked sunk) is now structural — flag-state screening mandatory at contract execution.

Sources: UKMTO Warning 104-26 (Hard T1) 3 Aug; UKMTO via FreightWaves (Hard T1) 4 Aug; Kpler via Foreign Policy Journal (T1) 3 Aug; Windward MIOC (T1/T2) 4 Aug.

Five-source Reuters report — Soft-tier (warning, not action) but corroborated by multiple independent sources.

The Iran GCC warning is a coercive signalling move timed to deter Bessent-deadline military follow-through. However, the IRGC's institutional separation from FM Araghchi means kinetic action could resume independently of FM diplomacy.

Implication: P(CENTCOM resumes by EOD 9 Aug) = 0.28, down from 0.35 — interceptor constraint is binding, not political. The primary trigger scenario for CENTCOM resumption is IRGC kinetic override of the deal window (e.g., vessel strike post-joint-statement). Monitor ISW factional-split signal daily.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
