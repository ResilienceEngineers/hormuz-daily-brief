Threat level

Day 204 of the US/Israel-Iran war: all three of Saudi Arabia's primary crude export pathways remain under simultaneous operational stress. The East-West pipeline (offline since 11 Sep, day 9) has not yet achieved its partial bypass target despite "within days" language from Bloomberg on 16 Sep; Kpler now puts partial restart ~one month out. Hormuz transits remain near historic lows (~8–12/day vs ~85 pre-crisis), with the IRGC having seized tanker Amara and fatally struck bulk carrier Minoan Dignity after the 60-day ceasefire expired. Houthi forces control Yemen's entire Red Sea coastline, Perim Island, and the Hanish Islands as of mid-September, giving de facto control of Bab al-Mandeb; US officials met Houthi representatives in Muscat on 14 Sep — Houthis pledged no attacks on US-flagged vessels but Saudi blockade continues. Washington declined Riyadh's request for direct military intervention. Brent settled at $103.21 on 18 Sep (−1.54%), with markets pricing partial pipeline bypass optimism; any Hard confirmation of bypass operational flow or Yanbu terminal Hard damage would move price materially in either direction.

Maritime Security

Why it matters: Both primary maritime corridors are simultaneously impaired at structural levels unseen since the war began. The US-Houthi deconfliction marginally reduces kinetic risk for non-Saudi traffic at BAM but does not restore Saudi oil export flow — the critical commercial variable.

Implication: Cape of Good Hope is the sole unconditional corridor. War-risk insurance premiums structurally elevated ($30–32/bbl VLCC in Persian Gulf). Any resumption of IRGC attacks on Muscat-pledged non-Saudi vessels would represent a major escalation requiring immediate hedge revision.

Implication: No full Hormuz reopening within 30 days. Sc A probability remains capped at 7% pending US political permission for Iran-Oman corridor. Flag-specific and nationality-specific carve-outs (non-Saudi at BAM, Oman-corridor at Hormuz) are the realistic near-term outcome space.

Infrastructure / Energy

Crisis ↑ Confidence: High
  • East-West pipeline: day 9 offline (10 Sep attack, Maysan-origin drones); bypass construction ongoing; Bloomberg (Hard T1, 16 Sep): "within days" = 17–19 Sep window now elapsed; Kpler (T1, 17 Sep): partial restart "within a month" (~2–2.5 mb/d); full capacity 6 weeks; no Hard flow confirmation 19 Sep 06:02 UTC
  • Yanbu: zero loadings since 11 Sep (Kpler T1); YASREF refinery operational (Industrial Info T2, 16 Sep); prior Houthi strike-on-refinery claims were disinformation using 2013 imagery (Industrial Info fact-check 16 Sep); satellite fires 15 Sep ~40MW each (NASA FIRMS T3) remain pipeline-related, not terminal strike; Aramco silent on separate terminal damage
  • Brent: closed $103.21 on 18 Sep (TradingEconomics T2, −1.54%); open $104.10 on 19 Sep (Investing.com T2); 18 Sep move driven by bypass optimism + Saudi STS workaround (satellite: ~2.8 mb/d Hormuz past 6 days); Goldman $120 tail active; Rapidan: Saudi exports −400k b/d this month

Why it matters: The pipeline is the structural inter-connector between Hormuz bypass (Abqaiq/eastern fields) and Red Sea loading (Yanbu). Its continued outage means Saudi Arabia cannot move eastern crude to western ports regardless of BAM status. The bypass timeline has slipped vs Bloomberg's 16 Sep estimate.

Implication: Brent $100–110 band holds while bypass remains unconfirmed. Hard bypass confirmation = Brent −$3–5 near-term; Yanbu terminal Hard damage (separate from pipeline) = $110+ floor. 24 Sep pipeline day-14 threshold is the next structural inflection for Threat-5 criteria review.

Alternative Routes

Crisis ↑ Confidence: High
  • Cape of Good Hope: sole unconditional corridor; European Saudi cargoes cancelled late Sep–Nov (Argus T2, 15 Sep); Saudi selling West African substitutes; Cape adds 2–3 weeks and ~$3–5/bbl freight premium vs Suez; no capacity ceiling yet but congestion watch active
  • Suez/SUMED: Saudi Yanbu–Ain Sukhna–SUMED–Sidi Kerir route collapsed as Yanbu loadings halted; Korea/Japan reroute via SUMED already disrupted (Aramco asked Sep allocations use SUMED, now broken); Suez northbound Saudi cargo near-zero
  • Ras Tanura STS workaround: satellite imagery shows Saudi Arabia moving ~2.8 mb/d through Hormuz past 6 days (TradingEconomics T2, 18 Sep); 60M bbl pre-sold for Sep/Oct loadings outside strait via STS; freight cost $30–32/bbl war risk; structurally insufficient to offset full pipeline outage

Why it matters: The triple-pathway simultaneous impairment (Hormuz, pipeline, BAM) leaves no route combination that fully compensates for normal Saudi export volumes. The STS Ras Tanura workaround partially offsets but at prohibitive cost and with Hormuz-risk exposure.

Implication: Cape unconditional through end-October minimum. Monitor Cape port congestion (Rotterdam, Algeciras, Singapore) as the leading indicator of alt-route saturation. If congestion triggers delays >5 days, this propagates directly into European refinery run-rate decisions within 2–3 weeks.

Sources: Kpler (T1) 17 Sep · TradingEconomics (T2) 18 Sep · Argus (T2) 15 Sep · Windward (T3) 11 Sep · Bloomberg (Hard T1) 16 Sep

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
