Maritime Security

Crisis ↑ Confidence: High
  • IMF PortWatch (T1) Jul 23: 10 vessels/day through Hormuz vs ~88/day pre-war — 11% of baseline; down from ~25/day late June; NBC/Statista confirm downward trend from brief Jul pickup.
  • TradingEconomics/Fars (T1, Jul 31): IRGC stopped two tankers from passing through Hormuz; four others altered course; war-risk insurance 8× pre-crisis, 6 P&I clubs withdrawn (straits.live).
  • AJ (T1, Jul 31): Kpler ship-tracking showed two VLCCs successfully exited strait on Friday — confirming selective Chinese/exempted-vessel passage corridor operating in parallel to general closure.

Why it matters: Hormuz is effectively closed to commercial traffic; the selective Chinese/exempted corridor creates a two-tier transit regime that further embeds Iran's chokepoint leverage and complicates any US-led reopening narrative. Transit at 11% of baseline with a downward trajectory since late June means no recovery in the 30-day window under current conditions.

Implication: Cape of Good Hope is the only structurally reliable route for non-exempt cargo. Every day without a Hormuz reopening extends the Cape utilisation curve and tightens VLCC availability. Insurance costs at 8× pre-crisis make spot transits economically irrational for most cargo categories; lock long-haul capacity now before Cape saturation.

Sources: IMF PortWatch (Hard T1) Jul 23; NBC News (T1) tracker; TradingEconomics (T1) Jul 31; AJ (T1) Jul 31; straits.live Jul 23.

Alternative Routes

Crisis ↑ Confidence: High
  • Kpler via Maritime Executive (T1, Jul 31): Four Saudi tankers attacked by Houthis since Jul 20; Bab al-Mandeb transit volume at multi-month low; Houthi declaration of maritime embargo on Saudi Arabia Jul 20 now operationally active with kinetic attacks.
  • Reuters / Insurance Journal (T1, Jul 29): Iranian advisers on ground with Houthi officials setting up Bab fee authority; Chinese vessels to be exempted; Houthi HOCC public denial Aug 1 (AJ/RFE-RL T1) is a legal/diplomatic cover statement, not an operational reversal.
  • TradingEconomics (T1, Jul 31): Saudi Arabia held talks with 43 nations on maritime coalition; no operational announcement yet; Saudi East-West pipeline to Yanbu now under Houthi interdiction — no functional Saudi export corridor exists.

Why it matters: Bab al-Mandeb was the primary Hormuz relief valve — Saudi Yanbu exports surged to ~3.5 mb/d in June as the bypass route. Now Yanbu is −40% from physical damage and Bab is under active kinetic interdiction. The East-West pipeline now delivers crude to a coast it cannot export from. Saudi Arabia has no functional export route. This is the structural significance of the dual-chokepoint scenario: it is not additive, it is multiplicative.

Implication: Cape of Good Hope is the only structurally reliable route and VLCC capacity is tightening as demand rises. Lock capacity now. 43-nation coalition formation (W4) is the primary downside scenario signal for Bab — watch for formal operational announcement as the first meaningful de-escalation signal on this route. Buffer recommendation: 150 days Cape capacity from today's date.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
