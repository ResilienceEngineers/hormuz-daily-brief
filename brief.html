Day 273 is defined by the first simultaneous confirmed attack on Saudi Arabia's Gulf and Red Sea refinery coasts: the Houthi projectile strike on Jazan (26 Nov) was Hard-confirmed by the Saudi Energy Ministry — 1 Nepali killed, fire triggered, partial ops suspended; Aramco's 400,000 b/d Red Sea refinery is the third confirmed Houthi attack on that facility since July 2026. The adjacent Ras Tanura event (25 Nov) remains in a harder-to-classify state: satellite imagery (Reuters/Planet Labs/NASA) shows thick black smoke, a CNN shipping source reports terminal loadings suspended, but Saudi Aramco has issued no damage statement as of 06:00 UTC 27 Nov — classified as "probable partial disruption, unconfirmed shutdown" per Day 272 methodology delta. UKMTO Warning 222-26 (26 Nov, tanker 50NM west of Dubai, projectile near-miss) and the 25 Nov Khasab tanker strike confirm the structural Hormuz incident cadence is maintained. Brent surged from $87.46 (25 Nov) to $96.61 (27 Nov), snapping a three-week losing streak, as the market re-priced dual-coast Saudi supply risk. Iran-US talks remain frozen; Vance's demand for concrete enrichment concessions continues to block any framework; Houthis remain ~60 km from Aden with the defense line unbroken.

Maritime Security

Severe ↑ Confidence: High
  • UKMTO Warning 222-26 (25 Nov): tanker struck by unidentified projectile 18NM east of Khasab in Hormuz Strait — crew safe, no environmental impact. Outbound transit. (Tier 1 — UKMTO)
  • UKMTO (26 Nov): unidentified projectile passed close to tanker ~50NM west of Dubai — crew safe, no damage. Near-miss in inner Gulf. (Tier 1 — UKMTO via Logistics Middle East)
  • UANI (Nov 23): US-facilitated transits surged to ~38/day (Nov 19–21); AIS-visible traffic running at ~9% of pre-war rate; war-risk insurance 7–9% hull value per transit (Lloyd's List, 9 Nov); cumulative campaign average 23.7/day since war outbreak. (Tier 2 — UANI)

Assessment: The structural incident cadence (≥1 UKMTO-confirmed event per 24–48h window) is fully maintained. The 25–26 Nov window produced two confirmed UKMTO events plus the Jazan refinery strike. The Hormuz corridor is operating at severely degraded throughput. The inner Gulf (W of Dubai) near-miss and the Khasab transit strike indicate both the northern Gulf and Hormuz narrows remain simultaneously active attack zones.

Supply-chain implication: Tanker shortages are compounding insurance cost barriers. Vessels avoiding AIS exposure are US-escorted only; unescorted commercial traffic remains extremely high-risk. War-risk premium at $4/barrel effective cost bakes into FOB pricing. Any additional Hard-confirmed UKMTO incident before EOD 27 Nov confirms cadence acceleration.

Sources: UKMTO (Tier 1) · UANI/Charlie Brown (Tier 2) · Lloyd's List (Tier 2) · Reuters (Tier 2)

Military / Kinetic

Assessment: The 25–26 Nov attack sequence represents a qualitative escalation: for the first time in the conflict, Saudi Arabia's Gulf coast (Ras Tanura) and Red Sea coast (Jazan) refinery infrastructure were struck within 48 hours simultaneously. This dual-coast pattern closes the East-West pipeline as the last unattacked export artery, making it the new highest-priority target watch. The Houthi operational tempo and geographic reach have demonstrably expanded.

Supply-chain implication: ~950,000 b/d of Saudi refining capacity under simultaneous confirmed/probable disruption. Distillate exports (diesel, jet fuel) from both coasts affected. If Aramco confirms Ras Tanura shutdown ≥24h, Threat-5 trigger criteria met. Coalition response (Hodeidah launch platform destroyed) demonstrates retaliatory capacity but has not degraded Houthi strike tempo.

Assessment: Diplomacy remains structurally frozen at the same impasse as Day 271. The dual-coast Saudi refinery attacks of 25–26 Nov have, if anything, compressed the diplomatic space further — escalating actions rarely open diplomatic corridors. The Qatar-facilitated framework has no Iranian buy-in signal. Araghchi's public characterization of talks as "proceeding intensively" is a Tier 5 (state communication) signal and cannot be treated as a Hard-tier event. The OPEC+ meeting is the only time-bounded diplomatic/structural decision in the next 72 hours.

Supply-chain implication: Continued diplomatic stalemate means Hormuz remains under CENTCOM escort protocol with no sovereign reopening. Insurance premiums will not ease without a framework signal. OPEC+ quota rollover (most likely outcome per delegates) would maintain current output levels — marginally bullish Brent given Saudi disruptions.

Infrastructure

Severe ↑ Confidence: High
  • Jazan refinery (400,000 b/d, Red Sea): partial ops suspension confirmed by Saudi Energy Ministry (26 Nov); fire contained; 3rd confirmed Houthi attack on this facility since July 2026; adjacent 2.4 GW power plant at risk. (Tier 1 — Saudi Energy Ministry)
  • Ras Tanura (550,000 b/d, Gulf): terminal loadings suspended (CNN shipping source, 26 Nov); satellite smoke visible from space; refinery damage neither confirmed nor denied by Aramco; "debris only" claim from coalition not independently verified. (Tier 2 — CNN/Reuters/Planet Labs)
  • Jeddah port (25 Nov) and Jazan port (25 Nov): Houthi claims on both Red Sea ports; Jazan port confirmed minor damage (coalition statement); Jeddah claim unverified by Hard-tier source. Saudi Arabia's entire Red Sea coast energy export infrastructure now under active threat. (Tier 2 — Arab News/Al Jazeera)

Assessment: The dual-coast attack pattern of 25–26 Nov is the most significant infrastructure escalation since the March 2026 Ras Tanura shutdown. Combined exposed refining capacity is ~950,000 b/d. The East-West pipeline (Petroline, ~5 Mb/d capacity) is now Saudi Arabia's only structurally intact major crude export route — making it the highest-priority watch item for further Houthi or IRGC targeting. The lack of an Aramco statement on Ras Tanura after 48h is unprecedented in this conflict and itself constitutes an operational signal.

Supply-chain implication: Middle Eastern refined product exports (already ~50% of pre-war volumes) face further compression. Distillate buyers in South Asia and East Africa most exposed (Jazan/Red Sea supplies). Crude buyers dependent on Ras Tanura terminal face immediate alternative-sourcing requirement. Insurance for Saudi port calls will spike across both coasts.

Sources: Saudi Energy Ministry (Tier 1) · Saudi Press Agency (Tier 1) · Bloomberg (Tier 2) · CNN/Reuters (Tier 2) · Arab News (Tier 2)
05

Alternative Routes

Elevated → Confidence: Medium
  • Bab al-Mandeb: Houthi coastal control (~150km coastline Dhubab–Khor Amira) consolidated after Yemeni government withdrawal 15 Nov 2026; Houthis ~60 km from Aden (AP/Reuters 24–25 Nov); defense line established but not tested under full Houthi assault. Red Sea/Suez route effectively closed for most commercial traffic. (Tier 2 — AP/Reuters)
  • Cape of Good Hope: Primary diversion route for non-escorted traffic; adds 10–14 days transit; fully operational but adding ~$2–3M per voyage in bunker/time costs; no incident risk but capacity saturation risk as more operators divert. (Tier 3 — industry)
  • East-West pipeline (Petroline): Saudi Arabia's only uninterrupted crude export artery after dual-coast strikes; ~5 Mb/d capacity; Yanbu Red Sea terminus is now the critical point-of-failure — if Jazan strike disruption extends or Yanbu is targeted, Saudi crude exports collapse. (Tier 2 — Bloomberg/ICIS)

Assessment: The alternative route picture has deteriorated materially. The Jazan refinery strike closes the Red Sea coast as a viable refinery-based export option. The Bab al-Mandeb Houthi consolidation maintains Red Sea/Suez as effectively closed. The East-West pipeline is now the critical single point of failure for Saudi exports — a Houthi or IRGC strike on Abqaiq-to-Yanbu segments would be catastrophic. No new functional alternative has emerged since Day 271.

Supply-chain implication: Supply chain managers should assume no Saudi Red Sea refinery throughput for minimum 3–5 days (Jazan) and uncertain Ras Tanura status. Alternative sourcing from Iraq (via Turkey/Ceyhan), UAE (via Fujairah), or US strategic reserves remains the only near-term buffer. SPR/IEA releases (40Mb scheduled Nov–Dec per ICIS) partially offset but cannot replace Saudi refining capacity.

Sources: AP/Reuters (Tier 2) · Bloomberg/ICIS (Tier 2) · UANI (Tier 2) · Industry shipping (Tier 3)

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
