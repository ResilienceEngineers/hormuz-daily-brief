The post-midterm decision window (3–10 Nov) is now open: Scenario B (Frozen Attrition) consolidates marginally to 48% as the MODAL case; Scenario A (Negotiated framework) rises to 18% on Qatari 3-day draft; Scenario C (Re-escalation) eases to 34% but remains a live tail on Netanyahu pressure and nuclear-ban debate.

Cargo owners and charterers should maintain elevated war-risk provisioning and avoid scheduling non-US-facilitated Hormuz transits. Any Bab al-Mandeb routing carries SUBSTANTIAL Houthi risk.

Implication: Scenario A probability raised to 18% on procedural progress, not substantive concessions. Do not reduce supply-chain contingency until the first CBM step is Hard-confirmed. A Qatar withdrawal from mediation or Iranian FM-level rejection of the 3-day sequence would collapse Scenario A to ≤5% and push Scenario C above 45%.

Infrastructure

Severe → Confidence: High
  • Ras Tanura: confirmed fire 30 Oct 2026 from Houthi 6-target salvo; operations resuming but terminal at elevated risk; East-West pipeline running at reduced throughput
  • Kharg Island: degraded from July 2026 US strikes; reduced crude export capacity; Iran relying on shadow-fleet corridor through northern Indian Ocean
  • Hormuz corridor: non-facilitated commercial traffic near-zero; US-assisted corridor functional at ~40/day; war-risk insurance structurally at 0.7–1.0% ship value; Fujairah operating as key alt-load bunkering point outside Hormuz

Why it matters: The Ras Tanura fire demonstrates Houthi kinetic reach into Gulf state energy infrastructure — not merely shipping lanes. The East-West pipeline reduced rate creates a bottleneck for Saudi Red Sea exports via Yanbu at precisely the moment Yanbu is the primary alternative to Hormuz-routed Gulf crude. A second confirmed Houthi hit on Yanbu terminal would trigger Threat-5 criteria.

Implication: Maintain Yanbu zero-load watch (W5) as highest-priority infrastructure trigger. Buyers dependent on Gulf crude should ensure inventory buffers cover 21–30 days of disruption. Petrochemical feedstock sourcing from Gulf origin should have alternate supplier activated, not merely identified.

Sources: UKMTO Update 110 (T1) · UANI Shipping Update 2 Nov (T3) · Africa Center Oct 2026 (T3)

Alternative Routes

Concerning → Confidence: Medium
  • Bab al-Mandeb: ~25 weekly transits (~3.6/day) vs 80/day pre-crisis; Houthi blockade on Saudi-linked ships structural since Jul 2026; UKMTO SUBSTANTIAL; 0.7–1.0% war-risk insurance; passage structurally impaired
  • Cape of Good Hope: primary diversion route; adds 10–14 days and ~$1.5–2M/voyage; no confirmed disruptions; capacity saturation risk rising as duration of Hormuz disruption extends into month 9
  • Suez Canal: reduced throughput from Houthi interdiction of Red Sea approaches; operating but below pre-crisis levels; diverted cargo adding congestion to Mediterranean ports (Alexandria, Damietta)

Why it matters: Both primary alternatives to Hormuz (Bab al-Mandeb/Suez and Yanbu/Red Sea) are structurally degraded by Houthi activity. Cape of Good Hope remains the only fully operational alternative but is approaching throughput limits for global tanker capacity. If Cape saturates — or if a second Houthi strike closes Yanbu — global refined product availability falls into immediate rationing territory.

Implication: Do not assume Cape route is a durable long-run substitute. Freight cost equilibrium is holding but fragile. Begin assessing Q1 2027 supply positioning now if Hormuz reopening does not occur by end-November. Petrochemical and LNG buyers should prioritise long-term Red Sea/Cape cargo bookings over spot.

Sources: Africa Center Oct 2026 (T3) · USNI News Jul 2026 (T2) · UKMTO Update 110 (T1) · Lloyd's List Intelligence (T3)

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
