Six categories

01

Maritime / Hormuz

Crisis ↑ Confidence: High
  • Kpler AIS: 1 vessel exited Hormuz on 23 Jul (VLCC New Giant, Iraqi crude/China) — lowest daily transit since conflict began; zero vessels entered (Iran International / Kpler, Tier 1–2).
  • IMO Secretary-General Dominguez: "very few ships, if any" transiting; ~500 vessels stranded in Persian Gulf with ~6,000 seafarers aboard (UN News / IMO, Tier 1).
  • War-risk insurance at 8× pre-crisis; 6 P&I clubs withdrawn cover; CENTCOM naval blockade: 12 vessels redirected, 1 disabled (straits.live / CENTCOM, Tier 1).

Why it matters: Hormuz is functionally closed to commercial crude, LNG, and refined products. A single Iraqi crude tanker with US military escort represents the residual of ~20 million barrels/day in pre-crisis flow. The 85 AIS-dark tankers suggest some suppressed flow that cannot be reliably quantified.

Implication: No near-term reopening signal. CENTCOM's 13-night pause on Wave 14 introduces ambiguity but does not change physical closure. Maintain Cape of Good Hope buffer at 60 days minimum. FM clauses on Hormuz-origin cargo remain fully triggered.

Sources: Kpler (Tier 2) 23 Jul; Iran International (Tier 2) 24 Jul; IMO / UN News (Tier 1) 23 Jul; straits.live (Tier 3) 25 Jul; CENTCOM.mil (Tier 1) 24 Jul.

Military / Kinetic

Crisis ↑ Confidence: Medium

  • Iran surged retaliation on 24 Jul: IRGC/Iranian Army struck Bahrain, Kuwait (Ali Al Salem AB), Jordan (Azraq), and Erbil with drones/missiles. IRGC claims ammo depot at Ali Al Salem destroyed; Jordan intercepted 5 missiles; Bahrain sirens; Erbil airport temporarily closed (Xinhua/CBS/JPost/GlobalSecurity, Tier 1–3). US/coalition damage confirmation pending.
  • CENTCOM struck IRGC Seyyed al-Shuhada naval HQ in Gilan (northern Iran) 24 Jul, expanding geographic footprint of US strikes beyond southern/coastal Iran (CBS News / Iranian state media, Tier 1 + Tier 5 corroborated).

Why it matters: The CENTCOM pause is the first operational ambiguity since the campaign began 11 July. Simultaneously, Iranian retaliation is escalating in geographic scope — Erbil, Gilan, and a Kuwaiti air base all struck in one 24h window — suggesting Iran is pursuing a multi-front attrition strategy regardless of US tempo. Pickaxe Mountain remains unstruckon Day 4 of a 7-day presidential-commitment window (heuristic P ≥ 0.65).

Implication: If CENTCOM resumes strikes on 25 Jul, the pause was operational. If sustained 48h+, it is a diplomatic signal and warrants Scenario B upgrade. P(Pickaxe strike by EOD 28 Jul) = 0.62 (↓3pp; window shrinking without action). P(GCC energy infrastructure kinetic strike within 72h) = 0.30 (↑2pp from confirmed Kuwaiti base attack).

Diplomatic / Political

Severe → Confidence: Medium
  • Trump: "They want to make a deal — I just don't think they're ready yet" (Oval Office, 24 Jul); said exit strategy is "military or diplomatic" and Iran is "getting more serious" (CNN, Tier 1). Simultaneously met advisers on "massive attack" options.
  • Pakistan–China reviving mediation track: crude fell ~4% on 24 Jul on reports of Pakistan/China joint diplomatic push; Chinese officials "increasingly concerned" about economic damage (Bloomberg / TradingEconomics, Tier 1). No framework text yet — watchlist item.
  • Counter-signal: Rubio said Iran is "begging for a deal but not ready to make one" (AJ 23 Jul); US Senate failed to advance war-powers rebuking resolution; House passed measure to limit Trump's war powers — domestic political constraint on unilateral escalation (CNN, Tier 1).

Why it matters: Day 148 contains the most genuine diplomatic ambiguity since the Iraqi ceasefire rejection on 23 Jul. The CENTCOM pause, Trump's "deal" framing, and Pakistan–China mediation signal together constitute the first coherent de-escalation cluster. However, no Iranian reciprocal signal has been identified — Iran's simultaneous drone surge on Bahrain/Kuwait/Jordan contradicts any back-channel softening.

Implication: P(New diplomatic framework by EOD 27 Jul) = 0.08 (↑4pp — first credible multi-actor mediation attempt). P(10-day ceasefire by EOD 25 Jul) = 0.04 (↑1pp — CENTCOM pause introduces minimal probability). Mojtaba Khamenei still unconfirmed; IRGC autonomous command posture unchanged.

Sources: CNN (Tier 1) 24 Jul; Bloomberg (Tier 1) 24 Jul; TradingEconomics (Tier 1) 24 Jul; Al Jazeera (Tier 1) 23 Jul; NPR (Tier 1) 24 Jul.

Infrastructure / Energy

Crisis → Confidence: High
  • Brent settled ~$97/bbl on 24 Jul (−4.0% d/d; Bloomberg Tier 1 confirmed ~$97 close). WTI ~$87.88. Still +30% past month; $27.50 above year-ago price (Yahoo Finance / TradingEconomics, Tier 1). Retreat driven by Pakistan–China mediation signal, not supply change.
  • US SPR drained ~25% in 17 weeks: 415.4→311.4 million barrels (EIA data via Daily Caller / ISW, Tier 1–2). Strategic buffer capacity significantly reduced; major IEA/G7 stocks release would require political coordination not yet signalled.
  • CPC Black Sea terminal remains suspended post-drone attack; ~80% of Kazakhstan's oil exports disrupted (Bloomberg/TradingEconomics, Tier 1). Three simultaneous supply disruptions (Hormuz, Bab al-Mandeb, CPC) remain unresolved.

Why it matters: The $4 Brent retreat is a mediation-signal reaction, not a supply change. Physical supply disruption is structurally unchanged: Hormuz at 1 vessel/day, Bab al-Mandeb interdicted, CPC suspended. SPR drawdown means US strategic relief capacity is 25% below the pre-war baseline — limiting the policy toolkit for price management.

Implication: P(Brent sustaining above $95 at EOD 28 Jul) = 0.72 (structural; mediation signal does not resolve physical closure). FM force-majeure review required on all long-tenor Gulf-origin and Red Sea cargo contracts. Review SPR-adjusted inventory targets — US buffer capacity ceiling is materially lower than baseline.

Sources: Bloomberg (Tier 1) 24 Jul; TradingEconomics (Tier 1) 24 Jul; EIA via Daily Caller (Tier 1–2) 24 Jul; Yahoo Finance (Tier 1) 24 Jul.

Alternative Routes

Crisis ↑ Confidence: High
  • Bab al-Mandeb: Houthi blockade on Saudi exports active since 20 Jul; at least 5–7 tankers diverted; Clarksons note: tankers loaded at Yanbu shifting toward Suez Canal rather than transiting south (Reuters/Clarksons via Daily Caller, Tier 1–2). Saudi Arabia responded with strikes on Yemen (CNN, Tier 1).
  • China-linked VLCCs still transiting Bab al-Mandeb: two VLCCs (combined 4 million barrels, Singapore-flagged) crossed 23 Jul per MarineTraffic data (Daily Caller, Tier 2). Selective enforcement pattern holds — non-Saudi, non-US-affiliated tonnage still moving.
  • Houthis fired missiles at a Saudi airport (34.1 AJ confirmed); UN Yemen envoy Hans Grundberg alarmed; Saudi Arabia now treating Yemen as an active front. Yanbu East-West pipeline remains commercially neutralised; no sign of reopening.

Why it matters: Both primary Hormuz bypasses (Yanbu/E-W pipeline and Red Sea/Bab al-Mandeb) are functionally neutralised for Saudi/non-China-linked crude. Cape of Good Hope is the sole uncontested large-volume route, absorbing all Western-market diversions. Congestion and insurance loading on Cape routes will compound cost pressure.

Implication: Cape of Good Hope buffer at 60 days minimum — maintain. P(Yanbu kinetic infrastructure strike by EOD 28 Jul) = 0.42 (↓3pp; Saudi Arabia now counter-striking Yemen, which may deter direct pipeline attack for 72h per Houthi calibration heuristic). Asian buyers discussing Cape rerouting for Saudi crude (Bloomberg, Tier 1).

Sources: UKMTO (Tier 1) 23 Jul; Al Jazeera (Tier 1) 24 Jul; Reuters via Daily Caller (Tier 1–2) 24 Jul; Bloomberg (Tier 1) 24 Jul; MarineTraffic via Daily Caller (Tier 2) 23 Jul.

Outlook / Scenarios

Crisis → Confidence: Medium
  • First genuine de-escalation cluster in 14 days: CENTCOM pause (night 14), Trump "deal" language, Pakistan–China mediation. Brent priced in 4% relief — market-as-signal assigns ~15% probability to near-term diplomatic progress.
  • Iranian counter-escalation simultaneous: drone/missile strikes on 4 GCC/regional targets (Bahrain, Kuwait, Jordan, Erbil) on 24 Jul is the broadest single-day geographic spread since the war began. Iran's strategic logic appears to be: accept diplomacy signals at political level while maintaining kinetic pressure to improve negotiating position.
  • Pickaxe Mountain window: 4 days remaining on presidential-commitment heuristic (EOD 28 Jul). Neither executed nor stood down. CENTCOM pause on Wave 14 does not confirm Pickaxe preparation — but cannot exclude it. Intelligence gap on target package composition.

Why it matters: The most important bifurcation point in 14 days is the next 48–72 hours: if CENTCOM resumes with a qualitative escalation (Pickaxe, power grid, bridge), Scenario C deepens to irreversibility. If CENTCOM pause is sustained and a diplomatic framework text is tabled, Scenario B probability doubles within a week. The structural supply disruption is unchanged in either path.

Implication: Scenario C remains modal at 88%. The 4pp shift to A/B reflects genuine but fragile diplomatic signal — not a structural change. Scenario review scheduled Friday; if mediation text not tabled by EOD 27 Jul, revert B back toward 5%.

Sources: CNN (Tier 1) 24 Jul; Bloomberg (Tier 1) 24 Jul; GlobalSecurity (Tier 2) 24 Jul; NPR (Tier 1) 24 Jul; Al Jazeera (Tier 1) 24 Jul.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
