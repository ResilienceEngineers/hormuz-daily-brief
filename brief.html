Diplomatic window still open — Iran-Oman framework "finalized" but no signed text by cut-off; dual chokepoint closure structurally unchanged. Confidence: Medium.
Threat level
4 / 5 · Severe
Iran's Foreign Ministry declared the Iran-Oman Hormuz shipping framework "finalized" late 7 August, with ABC News reporting the agreement would establish a 60-day temporary route — Iranian-controlled inbound, Omani-controlled outbound — but no signed joint text had been publicly released by the 06:00 UTC cut-off, marking the fourth consecutive deadline slip. Aramco's Q2 results (4 Aug) confirmed hydrocarbon output fell from 12.61 M to 9.46 M bpd Q1→Q2, the CEO describing "the most severe supply disruptions in company history," yet declined to provide asset-by-asset status for Abqaiq, Jubail, or Yanbu — sustaining the information hazard. Houthis escalated to the northern Red Sea on 5 August, striking the Saudi tanker Wafa off Yanbu with ballistic missiles and declaring all Saudi Red Sea export routes targets, threatening the East-West Pipeline's only viable export terminal. Brent has traded in the $82–86 range this week, significantly below Abqaiq-disclosure fair value, as markets discount the diplomatic signal while physical supply constraints remain structurally unresolved.

Maritime / Hormuz

Crisis → Confidence: High
  • PortWatch/IMF (Hard T1, 2 Aug): 2 transits vs. 73/day pre-crisis; AIS manipulation acknowledged — actual figure may be marginally higher but commercially irrelevant at this level.
  • CENTCOM (Hard T1, 5 Aug): Southern route "remains free and open"; >1,000 assisted vessel transits since April — but these are escorted military-coordinated passages, not commercial reopening.
  • UKMTO/Riviera (Hard T1, 3–4 Aug): Minoan Pioneer struck northeast of Khasab; crew abandoned; one seafarer unaccounted for; no party claimed attack through 06:00 UTC cut-off.

Why it matters: The 98% collapse in transits has now persisted for 162 days. CENTCOM's "1,000 transits" figure spans 3+ months and encompasses ~11 per day under escort — still less than 10% of pre-war commercial volume. Any Hormuz deal text does not produce immediate commercial re-entry: mine clearance, insurer re-entry, and IRGC verified stand-down are all prerequisites with multi-week lead times.

Implication: Maintain full Cape reroute posture. Do not contract Hormuz-corridor freight capacity based on deal text alone. Earliest credible commercial reopening scenario remains 3–4 weeks after signed text + observable IRGC compliance.

Sources: PortWatch/IMF (Hard T1) 2 Aug; CENTCOM (Hard T1) 5 Aug; UKMTO Warning (Hard T1) 3 Aug; Riviera Maritime Media (T2) 4 Aug; straits.live composite (T3) 6 Aug.

The Quds Force coordination disclosure (NYT) confirms the IRGC has deliberately kept its proxy network activation separate from diplomatic track — meaning a talks-collapse does not require IRGC authorization lag; Houthis and Iraqi militias can escalate independently within hours.

Implication: IRGC cadence quiet ≠ de-escalation. If Iran-Oman text misses EOD 9 Aug, P(IRGC kinetic within 48h) rises to 0.50 per heuristic. Monitor Qeshm Island explosions for Hard-tier confirmation — if confirmed kinetic, Threat 4→5 immediately.

Diplomatic

Severe ↑ Confidence: Medium
  • ABC News live blog (T1, updated ~20:00 UTC 7 Aug): "Iran says framework of Oman agreement finalized" — 60-day temporary route, Iranian inbound/Omani outbound; US official: "no approvals or permissions and no tolls or charges."
  • CBS News (T1, 7 Aug): "Iran and Oman are close to agreeing on new arrangements… but not for a full reopening"; Iranian-Omani proposal has "no tolls or service fees" per CBS sources; Trump: "a lot of progress had been made."
  • TradingEconomics (T2, 7 Aug): Iran retains stated positions — bar on US/Israeli vessels; 20% cargo-value penalty for violations; full reopening conditioned on US blockade-lift. US position: international waterway, no sovereignty grant. Gap persists.

Why it matters: "Framework finalized" is the strongest Iranian signal yet — stronger than "final stages" (5 Aug), "agreed in principle" (NBC/MS NOW 6 Aug). The 60-day temporary route structure is concretely confirmed by US and Iranian officials to ABC News, which is a Hard-tier observable. However, Iran's stated conditions (US blockade lift, bar on US/Israeli vessels, fee-based future administration) remain publicly incompatible with US redlines. The IRGC's exclusion from the delegation remains unresolved. This is the fourth consecutive deadline slip and the fourth "imminent" or "finalized" signal. Deadline-Slip Discount Heuristic applies: −40% on same-week release probability.

Implication: Scenario A at 36% (+2pp). Do not operationalize until: (1) Oman FM issues independent confirmation (not Iran FM only), (2) signed text publicly available, (3) IRGC observable compliance on AIS for ≥48h. All three required before any Cape-to-Hormuz routing switch.

Sources: ABC News live blog (T1) 7 Aug; CBS News live blog (T1) 7 Aug; TradingEconomics (T2) 7 Aug; IRNA/Baqaei (T5 — Iranian state, treated as position) 5 Aug; Bloomberg (T2) 5 Aug.

Infrastructure / Energy

Crisis → Confidence: High
  • Aramco Q2 (Hard T1, 4 Aug): Hydrocarbon output 9.46M bpd (Q2) vs. 12.61M bpd (Q1) — 25% sequential decline. CEO described "most severe supply disruptions in company history." Analyst asked directly about Abqaiq, Jubail, Yanbu; CEO did not provide asset-by-asset status. East-West Pipeline "continued utilization" — at nameplate ceiling.
  • TWZ/Houthi claim (T2, 5 Aug): Saudi tanker Wafa struck by Houthi ballistic missiles off Yanbu — first confirmed Houthi attack at Yanbu proximity. Houthi spokesman declared northern Red Sea operations "will continue and escalate" to close all Saudi export routes.
  • Yahoo Finance/IIR (T2, 7 Aug): Abqaiq processing capacity described as "single most strategically important piece of energy infrastructure anywhere in the world" — Day 13 without specific facility status. Price "not showing any real impact" — structural mispricing confirmed.

Why it matters: The Saudi export architecture is now threatened simultaneously at both ends: Hormuz (closed) and Yanbu (Houthi target declaration). The East-West Pipeline's ~4.5M bpd Yanbu ceiling already limits exports to roughly 60% of pre-war Saudi capacity. A successful follow-on Houthi strike at Yanbu's tank farm or loading infrastructure would collapse Saudi export capability to near-zero. Brent at $82–86 is structurally incompatible with this risk profile — the market is pricing diplomatic resolution, not the physical reality.

Implication: FM execute-level. Abqaiq disclosure remains the single highest-impact observable: Brent $98–110 within 24h of release. Yanbu follow-on strike = Threat 5 precursor. Review all long-tenor contracts with Saudi crude exposure for FM force majeure language immediately.

Sources: Aramco.com Q2 results (Hard T1) 4 Aug; Aramco earnings call/Investing.com transcript (Hard T1) 4 Aug; IndexBox/Tadawul (T2) 4 Aug; TWZ (T2) 5 Aug; Yahoo Finance/IIR (T2) 7 Aug; NASA FIRMS (Hard T1) 27 Jul.

Alternative Routes

Crisis ↑ Confidence: High
  • The Hill/Kpler (T1, 4 Aug): BAM transit dropped to 11 cargo vessels — lowest in months — following Houthi Saudi oil depot strikes. Three of seven oil vessels entered Red Sea; two loaded at Yanbu.
  • TWZ/Houthi claim (T2, 5 Aug): Wafa tanker struck off Yanbu — Houthi spokesman: "operations will continue and escalate in targeting Saudi oil vessels in the northern Red Sea, to close off every route and prevent passage." CNBC/JMIC (T1, 22 Jul): Houthi maritime blockade of all Saudi ports declared; missiles and drones deployed near BAM.
  • JMIC Advisory (Hard T1, 4 Aug): Cape of Good Hope ~236 vessels tracked; Suez near-normal northbound; Houthi northern Red Sea expansion now confirmed — Suez southbound increasingly at risk if Yanbu collapses.

Why it matters: The Yanbu terminal was the structural relief valve for the Hormuz closure — the East-West Pipeline's Red Sea outlet that kept ~4M bpd of Saudi crude reaching Asian and European buyers via Cape. The Wafa strike and Houthi declaration of northern Red Sea operations marks the first credible threat to this relief valve. If Yanbu is forced offline, the Cape route carries zero Saudi crude export volume; Cape becomes irrelevant as a mitigation tool because there is no Saudi crude to reroute.

Implication: Triple-chokepoint scenario (Hormuz + BAM + Yanbu) is now a credible near-term risk, not a tail scenario. Cape reroute remains the only operational path for non-Saudi Gulf crude but buffer is exhausted. Maintain Cape but assess Yanbu vulnerability as W2 gate — this is the new highest-priority watchlist item.

Sources: The Hill/Kpler (T1) 4 Aug; JMIC Advisory (Hard T1) 4 Aug; CNBC/JMIC (T1) 22 Jul; TWZ/Houthi claim (T2) 5 Aug; CBS News (T1) 7 Aug.

Outlook

Severe → Confidence: Medium
  • Signal FOR trend (diplomatic progress): ABC News/CBS News (T1, 7 Aug): "Framework finalized" — strongest Iranian signal to date; 60-day route structure confirmed by US and Iranian officials; Trump: "a lot of progress."
  • Signal AGAINST trend (Houthi escalation): TWZ (T2, 5 Aug): Wafa strike off Yanbu = first confirmed Houthi attack in northern Red Sea, expanding conflict geometry; Houthi spokesman explicitly threatened all Saudi Red Sea routes — structurally worsening the supply picture regardless of Hormuz deal.
  • Signal AGAINST trend (market mispricing): EIA STEO (Hard T1, 5 Aug): Brent forecast $74/bbl for Q3 2026 — EIA projects deal-and-supply-recovery scenario; actual Brent $82–86 reflects market caught between physical disruption and diplomatic hope. Yahoo Finance/IIR (T2, 7 Aug): "Price not showing any real impact" of Abqaiq damage — structural mispricing persists.

Why it matters: The strategic picture is simultaneously improving diplomatically (framework text imminent) and worsening physically (Yanbu threat, Abqaiq non-disclosure, triple-chokepoint risk). These are not offsetting — they represent a compressing window: if deal text is released this weekend, Scenario A rises sharply; if it misses again, the Houthi northern Red Sea expansion means Scenario C probability rises faster than in any prior deadline miss because the physical backstop (Yanbu) is now under direct threat.

Implication: Weekend signed-text release remains the single pivot event. Absence of text by EOD 9 Aug triggers: Scenario C +5pp (per heuristic), P(CENTCOM resumption within 72h) +0.07, and immediate Yanbu follow-on risk elevation. Supply chain posture should assume no Hormuz reopening before 1 September at earliest regardless of deal text.

Sources: ABC News (T1) 7 Aug; CBS News (T1) 7 Aug; TWZ (T2) 5 Aug; EIA STEO (Hard T1) 5 Aug; Yahoo Finance/IIR (T2) 7 Aug; TradingEconomics (T2) 7 Aug.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
