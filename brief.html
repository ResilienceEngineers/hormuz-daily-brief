01

Maritime Security — Strait of Hormuz & Gulf of Oman

Crisis ↑ Confidence: High (UKMTO Tier 1 + CENTCOM Tier 1 + IRGC/IRIB Tier 5 claim)
  • UKMTO triple-vessel incident (28 Nov): UKMTO reported an incident involving three vessels struck by unidentified projectiles while transiting eastbound through the Strait of Hormuz; all crew members were accounted for and the vessels continued their journeys, while damage assessments and environmental impact remained unknown.
  • IRGC mine claims (28 Nov): Iran's IRGC Navy claimed two oil tankers struck naval mines and caught fire while attempting to cross the Strait of Hormuz through routes Tehran considers unauthorized — one near the entrance to Khasab Port, the other along the eastern route of the Strait of Hormuz. CENTCOM stated US forces and their regional partners were in control of the Strait of Hormuz and claimed Iran no longer had an effective naval force.
  • UKMTO Advisory 224-26 (Gulf of Oman, 26 Nov): UKMTO reported an incident involving a cargo vessel and military forces in the Gulf of Oman; in advisory 224-26 dated 26 Nov 2026, citing military authorities; incident time remained unconfirmed with an investigation ongoing.

Why it matters: Attacks on commercial shipping in and around the strait have reached their highest levels since the conflict began, with more than 30 incidents reported in October and at least 22 in November as of the 22nd, per CNN's analysis of UKMTO data. The 28 Nov triple-vessel incident — regardless of mine vs. projectile attribution — raises the daily incident count and signals IRGC mine/weapon re-employment in the TSS eastern approach and Khasab entrance. Even if CENTCOM's denial of mine re-laying holds, projectile attacks on three vessels in a single day marks an escalation in intensity.

Operational implication: The Khasab port approach and TSS eastern lane are now a declared-uncertain zone. US officials previously confirmed the US Navy cleared mines from the Strait of Hormuz Traffic Separation Scheme, with over 100 suspected mines identified by underwater drones; Trump stated the US would destroy Iranian vessels laying additional mines. If CENTCOM confirms new mine-laying, Trump's destruction-of-mine-vessels ultimatum becomes the most proximate US escalation trigger. Route planning must treat eastern TSS approaches as "confirm clear before transit" until explicit UKMTO/CENTCOM advisory issued.

Sources: UKMTO Advisory 224-26 (Tier 1); CENTCOM statement via APA (Tier 1); IRGC/IRIB 28 Nov (Tier 5 — claim only); ANI/Tribune India 28 Nov (Tier 3 secondary); CNN/UKMTO incident count (Tier 2).
02

Military Campaign — Houthi / Saudi Energy Targeting

Severe ↑ Confidence: High (Bloomberg Tier 2 + Al Arabiya Tier 2 + ISW Tier 1 + Al Jazeera Tier 2)
  • Houthi cruise missile on Riyadh intercepted (28 Nov): Saudi-led coalition continued to deal with Houthi attacks on Saturday, November 28; on Saturday morning it intercepted and destroyed a cruise missile launched by Yemen's Houthis toward Riyadh. Houthi outlet Almasirah claimed 13 wounded.
  • Jazan refinery confirmed shut (27 Nov): An attack on Saudi Arabia's oil refinery in Jazan temporarily halted some operations and killed one worker; a projectile struck the 400,000-barrel-a-day refinery on the Red Sea on Thursday causing a fire; the Saudi Energy Ministry confirmed one official dead and several injured, the slain worker a Nepalese national.
  • Coalition retaliatory strike (26–27 Nov): Saudi Arabia has continued to conduct airstrikes on Houthi military targets; Saudi Joint Forces Command Spokesperson Major General Turki al Maliki announced on November 26 that Saudi forces destroyed 50 Houthi military targets, including ballistic missile launch platforms and weapons manufacturing facilities, in Sanaa, Hudaydah, and Saada governorates.

Why it matters: The Houthis have now conducted confirmed successful strikes on Saudi energy infrastructure on three separate dates within a seven-day window (25, 26, 28 Nov). The Jazan refinery was attacked in August and September, causing the complex to be shut — the 26 Nov strike is the third confirmed hit on the same facility, raising the probability of progressive cumulative damage beyond what a single-incident shutdown implies. Coalition retaliatory strikes have not halted the attack tempo.

Operational implication: Combined Saudi refinery disruption now reaches ~950 kb/d (Jazan 400 kb/d confirmed + Ras Tanura 550 kb/d probable). Aramco's nine-month crude output fell 13.7% in 2026 as pipeline and strait disruptions continued to constrain Yanbu loadings. Procurement teams holding Saudi origin barrels should activate alternative-sourcing protocols; the East-West Pipeline (Petroline) is the last intact Saudi crude export artery and is now a Threat-5 watchitem per Day 273 methodology delta.

Sources: Bloomberg/Rigzone 27 Nov (Tier 2); Al Arabiya/Al Jazeera 26 Nov (Tier 2); ISW Iran Update 27 Nov (Tier 1); Khaleej Times live blog 28 Nov (Tier 3); Saudi Energy Ministry statement (Tier 2).
03

Diplomatic Track — US–Iran Framework

Broken ↓ Confidence: High (CBS News Tier 2 + UNN 28 Nov Tier 3 + Arms Control Association Tier 2)
  • Iran FM rules out US talks (28 Nov): Iran's top diplomat on Saturday said there were no plans to hold talks with the United States despite growing concerns over a return to war.
  • Trump names next strike target (28 Nov): Donald Trump said that the United States would strike the facility on Mount Pickaxe in the event of renewed fighting with Iran, and called on Tehran to reach a deal.
  • Qatar mediation failed (prior weeks): Qatari mediators presented Iran with a new draft deal to end the war; a senior Iranian official told Reuters that Qatar had come with "unacceptable" ideas, and that Tehran would not change its stance on the nuclear issue.

Why it matters: The backdrop is a rapidly narrowing diplomatic window; Trump said on November 22 he was inclined to launch additional military strikes against Iran after the New Year if the two countries failed to reach an agreement, describing Iranian negotiators as "professional tap dancers" and expressing mounting frustration. Iran FM's rejection of talks on 28 Nov — the same day Trump names his next strike target — compresses the window to its narrowest since the conflict began. Signal against: Qatar has been facilitating exchange of messages between Washington and Tehran, and Pakistan has also played a significant role; negotiations center on Iran's nuclear program and the future of the Strait of Hormuz.

Operational implication: The effective diplomatic runway to Trump's post-New Year escalation deadline is now <35 days. Supply-chain teams should model a scenario in which US resumes strikes on Iranian nuclear/energy infrastructure in January 2027. This would likely produce simultaneous Hormuz escalation, rapid Brent spike above $105, and saturation of Cape of Good Hope route capacity within 2–3 weeks of resumption. Iran said the U.S. response to its proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and begin negotiations on a nuclear deal was insufficient, increasing the risk that conflict intensifies as both sides threaten to escalate.

Sources: CBS News live blog (Tier 2); UNN 28 Nov (Tier 3); Arms Control Association Nov 2026 (Tier 2); Gulf News live 28 Nov (Tier 3).

  • OPEC+ 29 Nov (TODAY) — 2027 quota decision: Two people familiar with the talks told Reuters the group is expected to keep output targets unchanged at least through the first quarter of 2027, with ministers yet to reach a decision; OPEC+ ministers meet on Sunday, 29 November 2026, to decide production policy for 2027.
  • EIA STEO forecast: The EIA STEO (Nov 2026) forecast Brent at approximately $97/b average for the period, expecting the price to remain near $100/b for the remainder of the year amid ongoing constraints.

Why it matters: The dual-refinery disruption (~950 kb/d affected) coincides with OPEC+ inability to deliver quota volumes: Gulf OPEC+ producers have been pumping well below output targets in the face of continuing export disruptions, with exports fluctuating at 60–80% of normal levels in recent months. A rollover today (most likely outcome) is marginally bullish because it signals OPEC+ will not add supply to compensate for Saudi disruptions. If Aramco confirms Ras Tanura shutdown tomorrow, Brent's move toward $100 becomes high-probability within 48h.

Operational implication: Iranian and American officials are still trading conditions over a Strait of Hormuz reopening plan, and the last quota decision carried the message: no fresh barrels on the horizon. Procurement teams should build a 72h hold on spot Saudi origin purchases pending: (a) OPEC+ 29 Nov result, (b) Aramco Ras Tanura statement. If both resolve bullish, forward-cover window is closing. If OPEC+ surprises with output increase, Brent downside to $88–90 range becomes viable over 2–3 weeks.

Sources: Investing.com (Tier 3); ICE (Tier 1); EIA STEO Nov 2026 (Tier 1); Bloomberg/Reuters delegates (Tier 2); Bloggoto/Reuters summary 28 Nov (Tier 3).
05

Alternative Routes & Supply-Chain Resilience

Strained → Confidence: Medium (structural assessment; no new Tier 1-3 route disruption event today)
  • Cape of Good Hope routing: structurally saturated. Red Sea commercial traffic remains near zero following Houthi consolidation of Bab al-Mandeb. Iran-backed Houthi rebels have advanced to within about 60 kilometers of Aden; the Houthis' advance came after government forces withdrew from areas on the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, allowing the Houthis to consolidate their grip on territory around the crucial waterway.
  • Taiz-Aden GLOC contested: Yemeni government-aligned forces conducted drone strikes against Houthi forces along sections of the Taiz-Aden GLOC on November 25 and 26; Houthi freedom of movement along the GLOC is necessary for the Houthis to achieve their primary objectives, namely capturing Taiz and Aden.
  • UAE Fujairah alt-loading hub remains functional but proximate risk elevated: UKMTO incidents within 92 km W of Dubai (26 Nov); Fujairah structural role as primary alt-loading point for Hormuz-diverted crude intact but under sustained threat environment.

Why it matters: The Red Sea closure and Hormuz degradation together represent a simultaneous dual-chokepoint disruption with no modern supply-chain precedent. Cape of Good Hope routing adds 12–15 days transit and ~$2–3M per voyage in additional costs. With Suez Canal throughput structurally reduced and Cape congestion building, the buffer capacity in the alternative route network is being consumed. Any Aden breach would sever the last Yemeni government-controlled foothold on the Gulf of Aden, further complicating naval escort operations.

Operational implication: Inventory planners should account for a structural 15–20 day lead-time extension for Gulf crude to European and Asian destinations via Cape routing. The Petroline (Saudi East-West pipeline to Yanbu) is the one remaining dual-coast bypass — but it now carries Threat-5 elevation per Day 273 methodology delta. Any signal of Houthi or IRGC targeting of Abqaiq pump stations or the Petroline corridor should trigger immediate escalation to Threat-5 watch regardless of Aramco statement status.

Sources: AP/ABC News 24 Nov (Tier 2); ISW Iran Update 27 Nov (Tier 1); UKMTO advisory 222-26 (Tier 1); Wikipedia 2026 Strait of Hormuz crisis (Tier 4 background).

30-Day Scenario Outlook

Deteriorating ↑ Confidence: Medium (probabilistic; structural inputs Hard-confirmed, scenario bounds inherently uncertain)
  • Scenario A (Negotiated framework) — 7%: Iran FM ruled out talks 28 Nov; Qatar mediation produced "unacceptable" response from Tehran; Trump names next strike target same day. Observable: public US-Iran joint statement + AIS-confirmed ≥3× transit increase.
  • Scenario B (Frozen attrition) — 15%: Requires diplomatic channel to remain open without active talks — but Iran FM's 28 Nov statement closes even that. Observable: diplomatic backchannel quietly resumes; Hormuz transit holds at 8–10/day without further escalation.
  • Scenario C (Re-escalation) — 78%: Trump said on November 22 he was inclined to launch additional military strikes against Iran after the New Year if the two countries failed to reach an agreement. IRGC mine claims, Iran FM rejection of talks, and Trump's naming of Mount Pickaxe as next target are all C-scenario confirmatory signals. Observable: US resumes strikes OR Aramco confirms Ras Tanura shutdown ≥24h OR Houthi breach of Aden defense line.

Why it matters: At 78%, Scenario C is now the operational planning baseline, not a tail risk. The question is not whether re-escalation occurs but which of the five converging Threat-5 triggers fires first. The most proximate is Aramco's Ras Tanura statement (now 4+ days overdue). The most consequential is US resumption of strikes on Iran after the New Year, which would drive simultaneous Hormuz escalation and Houthi intensification.

Operational implication: Scenario C supply-chain posture: (1) Treat Cape of Good Hope route as the planning baseline for all Gulf crude, not a contingency. (2) Build 30-day strategic inventory buffer above normal operating levels. (3) Activate alternative-origin sourcing for Saudi barrels. (4) Flag all long-tenor contracts with Saudi origin for force-majeure clause review. (5) Monitor OPEC+ 29 Nov result today — a rollover with Saudi disruption confirmed is the strongest near-term Brent-upside signal.

Sources: CBS News / UNN 28 Nov (Iran FM, Tier 2–3); UNN / Fox News 22 Nov (Trump escalation deadline, Tier 2); Arms Control Association Nov 2026 (Tier 2); ISW 27 Nov (Tier 1).

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
