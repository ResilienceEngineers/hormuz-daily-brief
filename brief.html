Day 155: The US–Iran war enters a renewed escalatory cycle as the White House confirmed fresh strikes planned for this weekend after Iran's IRGC struck US bases in Kuwait, Jordan, and Bahrain on 30 July. Hormuz vessel transits fell to 5/day on 31 July — Iran's Secretary of its Supreme National Security Council threatened to further tighten the declared closure; CENTCOM countered that the waterway remains open but acknowledged commercial traffic is "drastically" below pre-war levels of ~130 vessels/day. Bab al-Mandeb dropped to 11 commodity vessels on Sunday (Kpler), the lowest in months, as Houthi attacks on Saudi Red Sea infrastructure drove four Saudi tankers to turn back; Saudi Arabia held talks with 43 countries on a maritime coalition. Abqaiq processing complex remains offline with no formal Aramco throughput statement (Day 6 post-attack); Brent held ~$90–92 at open on 31 July before settling near $88, with the month recording a +22% gain — the largest monthly move since March 2026.

Six categories

01

Maritime / Hormuz

Crisis ↑ Confidence: High
  • CNN/Kpler (T1, 31 Jul): Only 5 vessels transited Hormuz on Thursday 31 Jul — all via Iran-approved northern route; two VLCCs exited successfully per Kpler despite IRGC claims of stopping two tankers.
  • CNN (T1, 31 Jul): Iran SNSC Secretary Zolqadr stated Tehran would tighten its declared closure due to US blockade of Iranian port shipping; CENTCOM countered on X that Iran "does not control" the strait and "thousands of ships" have transited over four months.
  • Reuters/TradingEconomics (T1, 31 Jul): US imposed sanctions on two Iranian firms supporting Hormuz "protection racket" on Wednesday; Trump confirmed new weekend strikes; White House: "Iran will continue to pay until they come to the table."

Why it matters: Hormuz at 5 vessels/day (96% below pre-war baseline of ~130/day) represents functional closure for commercial purposes. Iran's threat to further tighten the declared closure — even as CENTCOM disputes control — creates legal and operational uncertainty that keeps insurance war-risk at prohibitive levels and discourages all non-exempt transits.

Implication: Maintain Cape-only routing posture. Do not assume any Hormuz recovery in planning horizon. The CENTCOM/Iran dispute over closure is operationally irrelevant at 5 vessels/day — the practical closure is Hard-confirmed regardless of legal framing.

Sources: CNN (T1) 31 Jul; Kpler via CNN/TradingEconomics (T1/T2) 31 Jul; CENTCOM.mil (Hard T1) 31 Jul; Reuters via TradingEconomics (T1) 31 Jul.

The Qeshm civilian casualty incident gives Iran a propaganda justification for continued IRGC strikes while Trump's "beating" rhetoric locks the US into further escalation. Weekend strikes now confirmed-imminent by two US officials.

Implication: Apply updated heuristic: P(CENTCOM strikes this weekend) ≥ 0.80. Pickaxe Mountain nuclear strike — if executed — would constitute the single highest-escalation event of the conflict. Iran's stated response (treat as "expansion of war") would likely include Hormuz mining or Gulf state energy targeting. Immediate war-risk insurance review required on confirmation.

  • TradingEconomics (T1, 31 Jul): Yanbu loading −40% (5.16→3.09 mb/d) continuing; Ukrainian strike on Russia Volgograd refinery adds secondary supply pressure; US crude inventories falling.

If Aramco issues a formal throughput loss statement, all Saudi crude export mechanisms fail simultaneously — a supply shock without modern precedent. Market restraint ($88–92 vs. $98–110 model) suggests either SPR-release anticipation, demand-destruction pricing, or assessment that Abqaiq stabilization trains were not fully destroyed.

Implication: FM clause review must now move to contract execution level. Formal Aramco statement = immediate $98–110 Brent move; any Abqaiq restart timeline > 30 days triggers long-term supply-chain repricing. Maintain Brent structural floor at $88–92 absent formal confirmation; revise to $98–110 on Aramco statement.

Alternative Routes

Crisis ↑ Confidence: High
  • Kpler via The Hill / Ynetnews (T1, 28–29 Jul): Bab al-Mandeb fell to 11 commodity vessels Sunday — lowest in months; 7 oil tankers, only 3 entering Red Sea; four Saudi tankers (3.8 mb of crude, gasoil, naphtha) turned back pre-Bab; Hormuz at 3–7 vessels over weekend.
  • TradingEconomics / Reuters (T1, 31 Jul): Saudi Arabia held talks with representatives from 43 countries to form a maritime coalition to safeguard Red Sea shipping following Houthi blockade; no operational announcement yet.
  • Al Jazeera / S&P (T1, 23 Jul): Maritime insurance prices "soaring" for both Hormuz and Bab al-Mandeb — both waterways now theatres of war; JMIC warned Houthis "completed preparations" for shipping attacks with missiles and drones near Bab al-Mandeb.

Why it matters: Bab al-Mandeb was the primary relief valve for Saudi crude after Hormuz closure — Yanbu exports had surged to 3.5 mb/d in June vs. 240k b/d pre-crisis. That corridor is now under direct attack. Two-thirds of Bab al-Mandeb oil flow over the last two months was Saudi Arabia bypassing Hormuz. If Bab closes, Saudi Arabia has no functional crude export route.

Implication: Cape of Good Hope is now the only structurally reliable route. Lock VLCC Cape capacity immediately — 43-nation Saudi coalition signals Riyadh expects the Bab disruption to persist. Houthi fee regime (W3) formalization would make Cape the mandatory default for all non-exempt cargo indefinitely. Buffer: extend to 150-day minimum on fee announcement.

Sources: Kpler via The Hill (T1) 28 Jul; Ynetnews/Reuters (T1) 28 Jul; TradingEconomics (T1) 31 Jul; Al Jazeera (T1) 23 Jul; S&P Global (T1) 23 Jul; JMIC/CNBC (Hard T1) 22 Jul; Windward MIOC (T2) 22 Jul.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
