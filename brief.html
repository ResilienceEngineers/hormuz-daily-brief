Threat level
4 / 5 · Severe
Saudi Aramco restored the East-West Pipeline to approximately 7 million barrels per day on 16 September — day 5 of shutdown — providing the first material de-escalation signal since the 11 September drone attack; Brent fell 1.1% to $104.68. The relief is partial: Saudi Civil Defence issued a new Yanbu Civil Defence alert on 16 September morning (danger subsequently declared passed), 300,000 b/d Khurais field capacity remains offline, and the structural dual-chokepoint threat persists. Houthi forces now control Yemen's entire Red Sea coastline, Perim Island, and the Hanish Islands; BAM ships are still transiting but no formal closure has been declared, and Iran-Oman talks in Muscat on 15 September are proceeding toward a bilateral shipping-route framework that excludes the US and GCC. Hormuz remains effectively closed at 4–6 commercial vessel transits per day versus an 85/day pre-crisis baseline.

Maritime Security

Crisis → Confidence: High

  • Kpler: Hormuz commercial transits fell to 4 on Mon 15 Sep, down from 10 the prior day; straits.live confirms ~4–6/day vs 85/day baseline; war-risk insurance 40× pre-crisis; 6 P&I clubs withdrawn (straits.live, T3)
  • Yanbu Civil Defence alert issued 16 Sep AM for Yanbu — "danger passed" declared subsequently; no confirmed Yanbu port infrastructure damage as of 06:00 UTC (The National, T2)

Why it matters: Hormuz remains structurally closed to commercial shipping — only Iran-allied or exempt-flag vessels transiting with any regularity. The Kpler one-day drop from 10 to 4 transits is consistent with a new incident-suppression episode rather than a trend change. Yanbu Civil Defence alert on 16 Sep confirms Houthi strike corridor has reached the pipeline's Red Sea terminus — even without confirmed damage, loadings are episodically disrupted.

Implication: Maintain unconditional Cape routing for all non-exempt flags. Monitor AIS for new dark-tanker clusters at Hormuz. Any confirmed Yanbu port damage would trigger FM Declared status for all Saudi Red Sea–loaded cargo and erase the pipeline-restart relief.

Military

Crisis → Confidence: High

  • CENTCOM 9–23 Sep strike window open; no new confirmed US strike wave as of 06:00 UTC 16 Sep; Pentagon IG munitions shortfall ($21B request, production bottleneck) constrains optionality (DoD IG, Hard T1)

Why it matters: Trump denial of MBS Yemen airstrike request is a strategic constraint: US will not open a Yemen ground campaign, leaving Saudi Arabia to fight Houthi advance without the one kinetic tool that could halt it. Houthi Perim Island control is not merely symbolic — it places direct-fire artillery inside the BAM western channel. Pentagon munitions shortfall means CENTCOM strike waves must be rationed and sequenced, reinforcing IRGC and Houthi planners' view that US escalation capacity is constrained.

Implication: Houthi BAM control will persist through at least end-September. CENTCOM Yemen strikes remain possible but are politically constrained. IRGC escalation window is widened by the DoD IG munitions constraint. Any CENTCOM action in Yemen = probable IRGC retaliation in Hormuz.

Alternative Routes

Crisis → Confidence: High
  • Houthi territorial control: entire Yemen Red Sea coast, Perim Island, and Hanish Islands as of mid-Sep 2026 — structural BAM closure conditions met even without formal declaration (Wikipedia Bab al-Mandab crisis, 55-1)
  • BAM ships still transiting as of 15 Sep: Trump states Houthis "claim not to want a confrontation" and will let ships through — no formal Houthi closure declaration; practical threat persists (CNN/McGurk 15 Sep, T1)
  • Cape of Good Hope dominant; 466 Saudi-flagged vessels 99% in Saudi territorial waters (Windward 11 Sep, T3); Yanbu departure corridor under Houthi artillery threat from Perim; tanker rates at record highs

Why it matters: The structural situation at BAM is the mirror image of Hormuz: Iran controls Hormuz without a formal closure; Houthis control BAM without a formal closure. Both chokepoints are operationally restricted. Saudi crude now flows through the pipeline to Yanbu (restored 16 Sep) but must transit through a Houthi-controlled BAM to reach Asian/European markets. Cape routing adds 10–14 days and record bunker costs but remains the only reliable non-exempt-flag route.

Implication: Cape unconditional for Saudi/US/UK-linked cargo. Monitor Houthi political signals for formal BAM declaration. Formal declaration = Threat-5 trigger; Cape saturation begins; alt-route buffers breach within 14–21 days.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
