Maritime Security — Hormuz & Gulf

Severe ↑ Confidence: High
  • UKMTO W-214-26 (20 Nov): Tanker struck 8NM north of Khasab, outbound transit, projectile hit port bow, fire onboard, no casualties or environmental impact. Seventh UKMTO attack-class warning since 10 November. [Hard — UKMTO Tier 1]
  • UKMTO weekly report (published 21 Nov 01:35 GMT): Six verified attacks in and around Hormuz in the week to 18 Nov — four in the strait, two in Arabian Gulf. Hormuz traffic 65% below pre-war levels: 115 outbound + 148 inbound transits (week to 18 Nov), down from 139/169 prior week. [Hard — UKMTO Tier 1]
  • US official (20 Nov): Iran now deploying anti-ship cruise missiles against shipping in Hormuz — approximately one tanker attacked per day this month. IRGC fired anti-ship missile at US Navy-escorted vessels 20 Nov, prompting US defensive strike on Qeshm. [Hard — US official / CBS News Tier 2]

Why it matters: The shift to anti-ship cruise missiles against escorted convoys represents a qualitative escalation above drone/projectile attacks — it directly tests US Navy escort effectiveness and raises the probability of a US personnel-casualty event (Threat-5 gate). The 65% traffic reduction reflects that the structural dual-blockade is holding; the accelerating attack rate signals IRGC is not settling into a stable attrition posture but actively intensifying.

Implication: War-risk insurance premiums remain at crisis levels. Any additional VLCC strike, mine detonation on US-escorted vessel, or US KIA would trigger Threat-5 reassessment. Supply chains reliant on Hormuz crude have no near-term normalization pathway; alt-route and inventory buffers remain the operative resilience mechanism.

Sources: UKMTO W-214-26 (Tier 1); UKMTO weekly report via MEE/gCaptain (Tier 1/2); CBS News/National Security Journal (Tier 2); IRGC state media claim (Tier 5, corroborated by US official).

  • Lloyd's List Intelligence (Oct 2026): Bab el-Mandeb monthly transits reached 1,363 in Oct (+17% m/m); 63% higher y/y but 34% below pre-Houthi-crisis levels. Saudi crude piped to Yanbu creating inbound flow; Houthi naval ban on Saudi southbound tankers since 20 Jul 2026 constrains that corridor. [Hard — Lloyd's List Tier 2]

Why it matters: The dual-chokepoint compression is tightening: Hormuz is 65% below pre-war and under active attack; Bab al-Mandeb is 22% below pre-blockade under Houthi observation, with the port of Aden at growing risk. Saudi's East–West pipeline is the primary bypass, but Yanbu exits through Bab al-Mandeb — meaning both chokepoints are at risk simultaneously if Houthi advance captures Aden. Cape rerouting adds 10–14 days and 25–30% cost premium, with no near-term normalization.

Implication: Supply chain managers should assume Cape rerouting as the structural baseline through at minimum H1 2027. Yanbu/Red Sea terminal exposure should be reviewed given Houthi KAFD coercion pattern. Aden port operational risk requires active monitoring — loss of Aden as a transshipment node would cascade to Horn of Africa logistics.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
