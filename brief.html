Trump cancels "massive attack"; Iran–Oman Hormuz deal in "final stages"; US–Iran talks resume Mon. Confidence: Medium.
Threat level
4 / 5 · Severe
On 2 August, US President Trump announced he was cancelling a planned large-scale strike on Iran after Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, and reportedly Iran itself urged restraint, citing "parameters of a deal" having been agreed. Iranian Foreign Minister Araghchi simultaneously declared Iran–Oman Hormuz negotiations "in their final stages and on the way to being finalized." US–Iran talks were scheduled to resume Monday 3 August. These are the most significant diplomatic signals in 157 days of conflict, shifting the scenario calculus materially toward a negotiated framework — but Iranian state media gave no public confirmation of Tehran's request for a pause, and the Strait remains physically closed with IRGC force posture unchanged. Brent fell ~5.6% on the announcement to approximately $85 before partially recovering; the gap between market pricing and physical supply reality narrows but has not closed.

Maritime / Hormuz

Crisis → Confidence: High
  • Hormuz remains physically closed; IRGC force posture unchanged as of 06:01 UTC 3 Aug — diplomatic pause (announced 2 Aug) produces no observable AIS transit uptick within 12h window
  • Iran claims IRGC stopped two tankers attempting Hormuz transit (Fars News, Tier 5); Kpler AIS showed two VLCCs exiting successfully Jul 31 under escort — conflicting signals; net transit near-zero for non-exempted vessels
  • CENTCOM stated strait "remains open for transit" despite IRGC threats and attacks (AJ Jul 22); operational reality: Chinese-exempted and US-escorted vessels only; commercial insurance covers near-zero vessels

Why it matters: Diplomatic pause reduces kinetic-addition risk for 24–72h but does not move physical supply. The gap between "talks progressing" and "transit resuming" is measured in weeks of trust-building, not hours. Any party can collapse talks before physical reopening occurs.

Implication: Maintain Crisis status until AIS confirms ≥3× daily transit uplift sustained ≥72h. Do not treat Trump Truth Social posts or Araghchi statements as Hard-tier events — both are Soft tier (official statements, not observable physical change).

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
