  • Iranian state media / Globalsecurity (T2, 13 Sep): 1 Iranian seafarer (Jamshid Rajabi) killed, 4 wounded; 10 crew aboard; governor of Qeshm: "terrorist enemy" — attacker unidentified by any party. Trump: "I don't want to say."
  • Straits.live / IMF PortWatch (T3/Hard T1): Hormuz transits ~6/day vs 85 baseline (−93%); effective closure confirmed 14 Sep. CNN/IRIB: traffic ~90% below pre-conflict. MARAD-004 advisory in force.

Why it matters: The Qeshm vessel strike is the first confirmed attack on a merchant hull in Iranian coastal waters in the current tanker campaign window and occurred day before the now-postponed Salalah talks. Attacker ambiguity (US blockade enforcement vs IRGC false-flag vs third party) creates escalation pathway regardless of truth: Iranian attribution of US = diplomatic collapse accelerant; US acknowledgement = IRGC retaliation mandate under autonomous HQ doctrine.

Implication: Commercial transits will not recover absent a signed, operationally verified corridor with war-risk insurance clearance. The attack destroys any residual insurance-market confidence in transitioning from Cape back to Hormuz. Maintain Cape routing unconditionally; treat any single-source "corridor opening" signal as unconfirmed until UKMTO issues a positive transit advisory.

  • NPR / NBC News / Times of Israel (T1, 11 Sep): Houthis seized Mocha port (11 Sep), Mayyun (Perim) Island (11 Sep), completed full Yemen Red Sea coastline control; Houthi commander on video: artillery could strike waters off Yemen; ceasefire = territorial consolidation, NOT lane opening.

The Egypt pipeline/Suez Canal north-Red-Sea route is degraded but not closed — it is the only partial Saudi alternative, but with Yanbu receiving zero crude, even this route is moot for Saudi exports.

Implication: BAM formal closure watch elevated to W4 priority; declare all Red Sea transit contracts as FM-active or FM-watch as applicable. Cape routing: reserve capacity now before next CENTCOM wave or formal BAM closure reprices spot rates further. ADNOC Habshan–Fujairah pipeline remains the sole functioning Gulf crude export corridor — monitor capacity utilisation as a leading indicator of system stress.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
