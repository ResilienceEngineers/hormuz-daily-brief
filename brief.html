Day 261 maintains Trend Worse with Threat at 4/Severe. Iran's IRGC claimed three tankers hit mines in and around the Strait of Hormuz on 15 November — a third wave within 24 hours following two additional claims on 14 November, bringing the IRGC's stated 24-hour tally to five vessels; CENTCOM and UKMTO have not independently confirmed the 15 November incidents, consistent with the established IRGC propaganda-plus-partial-reality pattern. UKMTO's latest weekly report confirms Hormuz commercial traffic remains approximately 65 percent below pre-war levels, with 136 IMO-verified incidents since February and 25 seafarers killed. The UN Security Council convened 14 November at Bahrain's request to discuss Hormuz threats; Iran's deputy UN ambassador stated Tehran has "never closed the door to diplomacy" but set seven preconditions, while Rubio said further US military action is unnecessary as economic pressure continues. Trump signalled to senior advisers he may "hold off" on new strikes unless fired upon first (Reuters, 14 Nov), simultaneously sideling Vance from Iran negotiations — compressing Scenario A to 10% while Scenario C (re-escalation) remains at 48%, driven by imminent USS Roosevelt AOR arrival, Mossad chief Gofman's Washington–Miami visits pushing Netanyahu's war-resumption agenda, and an accelerating IRGC mine campaign.

01

Maritime Security — Strait of Hormuz

Severe ↑ Confidence: Medium
  • IRGC 15 Nov mine claims (3 tankers, 5 in 24h): Iran's IRGCN and Armed Forces Navy claimed three oil tankers exploded after striking mines in and around Hormuz on 15 November (ANI/APA); IRGC separately claimed two more on 14 Nov (APA.az 02:30 UTC), bringing 24h total to five vessels. CENTCOM/UKMTO had not independently confirmed as of 06:01 UTC — consistent with established 24–72h confirmation lag pattern. CENTCOM previously refuted multiple IRGC mine claims as "inaccurate" (57-9).
  • UKMTO weekly confirmed cadence: UKMTO week to 11 Nov confirmed 7 attacks including 11 Nov engine-room strike 14nm NW Ras al-Khaimah (MEE 14 Nov); UKMTO confirmed tanker incidents 10 and 11 Nov near Hormuz/Oman approach. IMO tracked 136 security incidents Mar 1–Nov 11; 25 seafarers killed (Al Jazeera 14 Nov / UN Khiari). Traffic 65% below pre-war.
  • Hormuz transit structure: UKMTO week to 11 Nov: 308 transits (169 inbound, 139 outbound); US-facilitated ~30/day; ~80% energy carriers. India-flagged exemption granted 4 Nov creates partial neutral corridor. Southern route under active IRGC mine risk; AIS-dark transits increasing.

Why it matters: The IRGC mine campaign has entered an acceleration phase — five claimed vessels in 24 hours represents a step-change in pace if even partially confirmed. The structural 65% traffic suppression is the longest-sustained commercial shipping disruption since the Suez Crisis. CENTCOM's mine-clearance claim (Aug 2026) is now contested by recurring IRGC mine-laying; the operational question is whether US mine-clearance capacity can keep the TSS route open ahead of Roosevelt's AOR arrival.

Implication: Non-escorted carriers face acute and rising risk on the southern Hormuz route. US-escorted convoys remain the only viable throughput mechanism. Any UKMTO Hard-confirmation of a mine strike on a US-escorted vessel is a Threat-5 trigger. Freight and war-risk insurance premiums on non-escorted VLCC routes remain structurally elevated.

Sources: ANI 15 Nov 2026 · APA.az 15 Nov · India TV News 15 Nov · Al Jazeera 14 Nov · UKMTO weekly via MEE 14 Nov · UN Khiari UNSC briefing 14 Nov · gCaptain 10 Nov · IranWire 4 Nov · ProxyDossier 13 Nov (UKMTO W201/W202 cites)

Military Posture & Kinetic Risk

Elevated ↑ Confidence: Medium
  • USS Theodore Roosevelt transit: Left Singapore ~9 Nov (The Week 9 Nov); National Interest projects on-station Arabian Sea "by end of November"; mid-November is now the central window. No CENTCOM AOR arrival confirmation as of 06:01 UTC 15 Nov. Joining USS George H.W. Bush and USS George Washington for three-carrier configuration — unprecedented since 2019.
  • IRGC Bahrain drone attack 13 Nov: IRGC claimed destruction of US early-warning radar in Bahrain (IRIB); CENTCOM confirmed two one-way attack drones launched, both intercepted, no damage (Euronews via AND Magazine 14 Nov). Attack framed as retaliation for Sirik telecommunications tower strike. CENTCOM response: no escalation; rhetorical signal value only.
  • Trump "hold off" signal: Reuters 14 Nov reports Trump signalled to senior advisers he may refrain from new strikes unless Tehran or Ansarallah fires first. Rubio (Federalist Society, 14 Nov): further US military action "unnecessary" given economic pressure. Counter-signal: Trump told Salem News Channel "if I saw things going the other way, I would do that…right now" — conditional escalation language maintained.

Why it matters: The Roosevelt's arrival opens the three-carrier Threat-5 window structurally. Trump's "hold off" posture is a soft brake, not a hard commitment — it is explicitly conditioned on Iran/Houthi not firing first. Israeli unilateral action risk remains the primary wildcard: Gofman's Washington visit shows Netanyahu is actively lobbying for resumed kinetics, and the Eisenkot constraint is political, not military.

Implication: Supply chain planners should treat the period 15–25 Nov as elevated kinetic-trigger window. Roosevelt AOR confirmation + any Iranian kinetic provocation (mine on US-escorted vessel, Gulf-state infrastructure hit) = immediate Threat-5 scenario. The Trump "hold off" posture narrows the window for self-initiated US action but does not remove Israeli unilateral escalation risk.

Sources: The Week India 9 Nov · National Interest 10 Nov · Axios/Barak Ravid 5 Nov (Singapore stop) · Reuters 14 Nov (Trump "hold off") · AND Magazine 14 Nov (IRGC Bahrain/CENTCOM) · Salem News Channel 13 Nov (Trump conditional language)

Implication: No diplomatic resolution pathway is structurally viable within 30 days. The Trump–Putin–Xi China trilateral is a macro wildcard — a joint statement with de-escalation language would be a rare Sc A signal. Supply chain contracts with 30-day Iran-normalisation scenarios should be extended or repriced.

Energy Infrastructure

Disrupted → Confidence: Medium
  • Gulf oil exports partial recovery: Gulf exports (ex-Oman) reached 18.4 mb/d in October — 74% of pre-war level, within 26% of baseline (gCaptain/Kpler 13 Nov). Vessel traffic through Hormuz remains 65% below pre-conflict; discrepancy reflects pipeline + non-Hormuz routing. Saudi exports dominate recovery via Red Sea (Yanbu) and East-West pipeline.
  • Houthi East-West pipeline/Yanbu claim (12 Nov): Houthis claimed drone and missile strikes on Saudi East-West PS7 and Yanbu; Saudi Arabia has not confirmed damage; coalition intercepted 2 drones Eastern Province/Riyadh area 12 Nov. No Kpler zero-load observation at Yanbu. Assessed: claim credibility low-to-medium — Saudi silence is inconsistent with major infrastructure hit; satellite imagery pending.
  • IRGC mine campaign and Iranian oil blockade: US naval blockade on Iranian ports continues; Iranian oil revenue near-zero (USD/IRR structural depreciation). Iran's mine campaign targets US-facilitated transit corridor — operational objective is to demonstrate CENTCOM's mine-clearance claim (Aug 2026) is insufficient. IAEA: secretive Isfahan facility uranium stockpile unverifiable (Ynet 14 Nov).

Why it matters: The partial Gulf export recovery to 74% pre-war represents the structural ceiling under current Sc B attrition. Any confirmed Yanbu physical damage cascades to Red Sea export disruption and would trigger a Kpler zero-load watch. Iran's mine campaign at Hormuz is designed to test whether the southern route US-cleared corridor holds — a confirmed hit on a US-escorted vessel would be simultaneously a kinetic and infrastructure crisis.

Implication: Maintain Yanbu satellite imagery watch through EOD 17 Nov. Gulf export recovery is real but fragile — Houthi operational reach to East-West infrastructure is confirmed in intent if not effect. Refined product supply chains dependent on Saudi Red Sea exports should maintain 7–10 day buffer inventory posture.

Sources: gCaptain/Kpler via UANI 13 Nov · Saudi coalition intercept reports 12 Nov · OPB 14 Nov (Iran conditions) · Ynet 14 Nov (IAEA Isfahan) · ProxyDossier 13 Nov (Yanbu unconfirmed)

Alternative Routes & Chokepoints

Strained → Confidence: High
  • Bab al-Mandeb — traffic decline, no confirmed attacks: JMIC advisory 12 Nov: BaM commercial traffic averaged 23 transits/day 8–11 Nov, down from 32/day prior week; 24% below pre-20 Jul Houthi embargo baseline; historical baseline ~61/day (2023). No confirmed attacks in period. Threat level: SUBSTANTIAL. Houthis hold Mayyun Island since 11 Sep 2026 — firing positions on both channels; ground offensive by Yemeni government forces ongoing near Dhubab–Mocha.
  • Houthi transit permit system active: WSJ 6 Nov reports shipping operators emailing Houthi HOCC for transit assurances; Chinese container ship Zhong Cheng Chang Ling transited Sep with one-week permit. Non-Saudi, non-US/UK/Israeli operators can seek assurances. Saudi-linked cargo remains targeted — Houthi embargo selective but enforced. Some operators AIS-on per Houthi instruction.
  • Suez Canal partial recovery; Cape of Good Hope dominant: Windward 13 Nov: Suez southbound 66 crossings (up from 41); BaM 39 crossings (20 inbound, 19 outbound). Suez 39% below pre-crisis but climbing. Cape of Good Hope routing remains primary alternative for vessels avoiding both Hormuz and BaM risk — adding 10–14 days transit time per voyage.

Why it matters: The BaM traffic decline from 32 to 23/day in one week is a material deterioration — driven by Houthi Mayyun Island control and uncertainty over Saudi-cargo targeting. The Houthi permit system creates a two-tier transit environment: Chinese/Indian/neutral flag operators can transit (at risk and under Houthi terms); Saudi/US/Israeli-linked cargo cannot. This structural bifurcation will persist regardless of Hormuz trajectory.

Implication: Cape of Good Hope routing costs are the new structural baseline for Saudi-linked and US/UK/Israeli-flagged cargo. Suez partial recovery is real but limited to non-sanctioned flagging. Operators relying on BaM transit should maintain Houthi HOCC notification protocols for non-restricted cargoes; Saudi supply chains should treat Red Sea export corridor as degraded through Nov 2026.

Sources: JMIC UKMTO advisory 113, 12 Nov 2026 (direct PDF) · UKMTO weekly via MEE 7 Nov · Windward 13 Nov · WSJ/Jerusalem Post 6 Nov (Houthi HOCC) · ACLED Oct 2026 (Mayyun/Houthi gains) · HouseOfSaud Oct 2026 (Mayyun 47% BaM) · CNN 4 Oct (Yemeni govt offensive)

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
