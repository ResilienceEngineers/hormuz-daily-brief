Day 272 is defined by the Houthi strike on Ras Tanura (25 Nov), Saudi Arabia's largest refinery at 550,000 b/d, with satellite imagery showing thick black smoke and OSINT analysts noting flaring had stopped — a curtailment indicator — though the Saudi-led coalition confirmed only four drones downed and did not confirm refinery damage. Simultaneously, UKMTO confirmed an explosion from an unknown projectile near a tanker 50 nautical miles west of Dubai (25 Nov, 21:30 UTC), with crew safe and no vessel damage — the latest in an unbroken sequence of Gulf maritime incidents. On Yemen's southern front, Houthi forces have consolidated at approximately 60 km north/west of Aden; the Yemeni army has established a fortified defense line, but government forces withdrew from the Bab el-Mandeb Strait coastline to defend the interim capital. Iran's seven-precondition posture remains unchanged; talks are frozen and Brent surged to ~$91 on the Ras Tanura event, partially reversing November's supply-glut decline, with the OPEC+ 29 Nov framework meeting now the principal macro downside risk.

01

Maritime Security

Severe ↑ Confidence: High
  • UKMTO advisory (25 Nov, 23:29 GMT): explosion from unknown projectile near tanker ~50NM west of Dubai; crew safe, no vessel damage, no environmental impact — classified as suspicious activity. Source: UKMTO/Reuters/AA/Arab News.
  • UKMTO Warning 218-26 (22 Nov, 10:26 UTC): bulk carrier struck on port side by unknown projectile while anchored at Dibba OPL, 23NM NE of Khor Fakkan UAE; fire onboard, no casualties. Source: UKMTO.
  • UKMTO Warning 214-26 (20 Nov) remains the most recent confirmed tanker attack: tanker struck on port bow by unknown projectile 8NM north of Khasab, fire onboard, no casualties. Structural cadence: ~6–7 UKMTO warnings/week since late October. Source: UKMTO.

Why it matters: The Dubai-area explosion extends the confirmed threat surface from the Hormuz/Khasab axis into the inner Arabian Gulf, 50NM from one of the world's busiest port complexes. Combined with Dibba OPL (Gulf of Oman outer anchorage), the operational risk perimeter has expanded materially. IRGC mine and projectile enforcement is no longer confined to the strait choke zone.

Implication: War-risk insurance premiums will not ease without a confirmed reduction in UKMTO incident rate. Inner Gulf anchorages (Dibba, Khor Fakkan OPL) must now carry elevated risk surcharge. Any vessel transiting Dubai-area waters should treat the 50NM W corridor as an active incident zone. STS operations in Gulf of Oman remain highest-risk; consider alternative STS windows in Fujairah or Red Sea if operationally viable.

Sources: UKMTO Warning 218-26; UKMTO advisory 25 Nov (Anadolu Agency, Arab News, Middle East Eye, Reuters); UKMTO Warning 214-26.
02

Military / Kinetic

Severe ↑ Confidence: Medium-High
  • Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree claimed drone/missile strikes on Ras Tanura refinery (25 Nov); satellite imagery (Planet Labs/NASA via Reuters) shows thick black smoke over the complex; OSINT analysts note flaring has stopped — a shutdown/curtailment indicator. Saudi coalition confirmed 4 drones downed but did NOT confirm refinery hit. Source: Reuters, The National, Energy News Beat, JBizNews.
  • Houthis struck Riyadh financial district in second wave (25 Nov); Saudi civil defence image showed renewed facade damage to PIF tower. Saudi air defences downed missiles/drones targeting Riyadh morning of 26 Nov. Coalition also confirmed projectile hit Jazan Red Sea port (minor damage). Source: Korea Herald/Reuters/Brecorder.
  • Houthi forces have advanced to within ~60 km of Aden; AP/Reuters/AFP (25 Nov) confirm government forces withdrew from Bab el-Mandeb Strait coastline (15 Nov) to establish defensive line north and west of Aden. Yemeni army spokesman Col. al-Nuzaily confirmed defense line "built to stop the Houthi advance." Source: AP, Reuters, Daily Sabah, Review-Journal.

Why it matters: The Ras Tanura event (25 Nov) is the most consequential Saudi energy infrastructure attack since the October 28 ballistic missile strike that briefly pushed Brent above $114. Even without confirmed shutdown, the OSINT flaring stop and satellite smoke constitute strong circumstantial evidence of at least partial curtailment of a 550,000 b/d facility. The Houthi pattern — simultaneous attacks on refinery, financial centre, and Red Sea port — signals a deliberate multi-domain pressure campaign timed to the OPEC+ 29 Nov meeting.

Implication: Distillate-exposed supply chains (diesel, jet fuel, bunker fuel) should activate contingency sourcing within 24–48h of Aramco confirmation. The Aden defensive line is not a stabilization — it is a rearguard position after strategic retreat. Any Houthi breach of that line within 10–14 days would eliminate the last defensible buffer before the port. CENTCOM Chabahar attribution (22 Nov) remains silent at 96h+; pattern suggests quiet enforcement, not attribution — reduces Iranian retaliation clock pressure but does not eliminate it.

Sources: Reuters (25 Nov Ras Tanura); Planet Labs/NASA satellite imagery; Maritime Executive (OSINT flaring analysis); AP/Reuters (Aden advance 25 Nov); Korea Herald (26 Nov Riyadh); JBizNews (Saudi coalition statement); The National (25 Nov).

Diplomatic

Critical → Confidence: High
  • Iran parliament speaker and chief negotiator Ghalibaf (24 Nov): Tehran will not negotiate with the US "until Iran's seven conditions are met." Conditions include lifting of US naval blockade, sanctions relief, release of frozen assets, formal declaration of war's end. Source: Iranian state television/WSLS/Gulf News.
  • Atlantic Council analysis (26 Nov): "Nine months after Operation Epic Fury began, the United States still enforces a blockade of the Strait of Hormuz while talks with Iran remain frozen." 71% of Americans in Reuters/Ipsos polling want new Congress to end the war. Source: Atlantic Council/Memesita.
  • OPEC+ 29 Nov Vienna ministerial: 42nd full OPEC+ meeting to set 2027 output framework; no authorized baseline after 31 Dec 2026; Iraq disputes capacity assessment (claims 5.2 Mbd vs consultant <5 Mbd); Kazakhstan and Russia may follow. UAE no longer in group (exit 1 May 2026). Source: Forbes, Rigzone, Admiral Markets, World Oil Monitor.

Why it matters: Iran's seven-precondition posture has not moved in over three weeks despite Qatar channel activity and Vance/Rubio Qatar meetings. The diplomatic clock is structurally frozen. The OPEC+ meeting is the nearest near-term macro catalyst — a failure to agree on 2027 quotas or a surprise capacity increase from Iraq/Kazakhstan would add bearish Brent pressure, complicating both Iranian and Saudi fiscal calculus simultaneously.

Implication: No diplomatic resolution signal within 30 days; baseline is extended frozen attrition with re-escalation tail. OPEC+ outcome on 29 Nov is a price catalyst in either direction: deep restraint → Brent $95+; capacity increase → Brent $82–85. For long-tenor procurement contracts expiring Q1 2027, the absence of an authorized OPEC+ framework after 31 Dec introduces a structural pricing uncertainty independent of the Iran conflict.

Sources: WSLS/Gulf News (Ghalibaf 24 Nov); Atlantic Council/Memesita (26 Nov); Forbes/Rigzone/Admiral Markets/World Oil Monitor (OPEC+ 29 Nov preview); Al Jazeera (Iran seven conditions framework, 12 Nov).
04

Infrastructure

Severe ↑ Confidence: Medium
  • Ras Tanura refinery (550,000 b/d; ~16% Saudi refining capacity): Houthi strike claimed 25 Nov; satellite imagery shows smoke cloud; OSINT analysts (Maritime Executive) report flaring stopped — indicating shutdown or significant curtailment. Saudi coalition says 4 drones downed; refinery hit NOT confirmed by coalition or Aramco. Per March 2026 precedent, prior attack caused ~11-day outage. Source: Reuters/Planet Labs/Maritime Executive/JBizNews.
  • Jazan Red Sea port: coalition confirmed projectile hit causing minor damage, no civilian injuries (25 Nov). Jazan is a secondary Saudi product export terminal; partial disruption adds to Red Sea throughput constraints. Source: Reuters/Brecorder.
  • Riyadh financial district (PIF/Kingdom Tower): third Houthi strike wave; satellite/civil defence images show cumulative facade damage. Primarily symbolic but signals Houthi long-range capability persistence and Saudi domestic vulnerability. Source: Korea Herald/Brecorder.

Why it matters: Ras Tanura is structurally irreplaceable in the short term — it processes ~550,000 b/d of products (diesel, jet fuel, gasoline, bunker fuel) feeding European and Asian markets already in deficit from the 9-month Hormuz crisis. The March 2026 attack caused an ~11-day outage that spiked Brent and triggered propane/butane export halts for weeks. A repeat shutdown would compound the global distillate shortage at a point when refineries globally have been running flat-out with extended service intervals.

Implication: Trigger contingency sourcing protocols for diesel and jet fuel within 24h of Aramco confirmation. Downstream logistics (Yanbu pipeline → Red Sea export) now must absorb both: (1) Ras Tanura potential curtailment and (2) Jazan port damage. Activate alternate distillate source contracts (US Gulf Coast, Rotterdam, Singapore) on a watch basis now; convert to execution if Aramco issues shutdown statement or 48h of continued OSINT flaring silence.

Sources: Reuters/Planet Labs satellite imagery (25 Nov); Maritime Executive OSINT (flaring analysis); JBizNews (Saudi coalition statement); The National (25 Nov); Wikipedia/PST International (Ras Tanura refinery data); Media Line/Reuters (March 2026 precedent).
05

Alternative Routes

Elevated ↑ Confidence: Medium-High
  • Bab el-Mandeb risk structurally elevated: Yemeni gov't forces completed withdrawal from strait coastline area (15 Nov) to defend Aden; Houthis now ~60 km from city with forces encircling N and E; Houthis threatening to strike Jeddah and other Saudi Red Sea ports. Source: AP/Reuters/Daily Sabah/Archyde (25–26 Nov).
  • Jazan Red Sea port struck 25 Nov (minor damage confirmed). Houthis separately claimed Jeddah port struck — no independent confirmation. Coalition conducting strikes on Hodeidah, triggering Houthi counter-threat to all Red Sea ports. Source: Reuters/Brecorder/Sightmagazine.
  • Cape of Good Hope route remains operationally clear but structurally saturated: absorbing combined Hormuz + Red Sea diversion for 9 months; freight premium persistent; buffer thin with no slack capacity. No new Cape route incident signals found. Source: ICIS/Rystad (structural assessment).

Why it matters: The Bab el-Mandeb situation has deteriorated significantly in 10 days. The government withdrawal from the strait coastline removes the only land-based buffer between Houthi positions and the waterway itself. Even without a formal Houthi strait closure, their proximity to Aden and control of surrounding terrain creates interdiction risk for southbound Red Sea traffic. The Jazan port strike and Jeddah threat — if confirmed — would directly impact Saudi East-West pipeline offtake at the Red Sea terminus.

Implication: Routing decisions for Cape vs. Red Sea must now account for Bab el-Mandeb as a secondary chokepoint under active threat rather than a disrupted-but-open corridor. Jeddah port watch status should be elevated. East-West pipeline product flows to Yanbu should be monitored for any Jazan port capacity degradation that shifts volume and creates terminal congestion. If Houthi advance breaches Aden defensive line within 14 days, Red Sea corridor should be treated as equivalent-risk to Hormuz for routing decisions.

Sources: AP/Reuters (Houthi 60km Aden, 25 Nov); Daily Sabah (Bab el-Mandeb withdrawal); Archyde/Reuters (defensive line); Reuters/Brecorder (Jazan port hit, Jeddah threat); ICIS (Cape route assessment); Rystad Energy (global liquids surplus/alt-route buffer).

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
