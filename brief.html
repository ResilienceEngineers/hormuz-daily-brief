5 / 5 · Crisis
A mutual 48-hour operational pause between the US and Iran — the longest since fighting resumed in July — has opened the highest-stakes diplomatic window since the June MoU, with Netanyahu travelling to Washington for a Trump summit on 29 July focused on Iran strategy. However, the Houthi front is escalating independently: Jizan Aramco refinery was struck and ignited, Saudi defenses intercepted two ballistic missiles targeting Yanbu (the kingdom's sole Hormuz-bypass crude export terminal), and Bab al-Mandeb commercial transits fell to 11 vessels on 26 July — the lowest in months. Brent dropped sharply to $90.95 on the diplomatic signal, undermining the $95 structural floor assumed in prior assessments. The core bifurcation now sharpens: the US–Iran axis is showing its most credible de-escalation signal in 5 weeks, while the Houthi proxy axis is independently escalating Saudi energy infrastructure — a dynamic that could derail any ceasefire framework.

Six categories

01

Maritime / Hormuz

Crisis → Confidence: High
  • Transit suppression sustained: IMF PortWatch data shows ~25 vessels/day in late June–early July, declining to ~10/day by the week of Jul 13–19 — vessel traffic remains nowhere near the hundred-plus ships a day that were traversing the strait before the war .
  • Mutual operational pause — no AIS recovery yet: The United States paused attacks on Iran for a second straight day Sunday and Tehran said it had done so too, as efforts continued to bring the countries back to negotiations on an interim ceasefire . No verifiable increase in commercial transits yet confirmed.
  • Iran–Oman technical talks — no change: Iranian state media claims Tehran and Muscat held constructive technical talks on Hormuz safe-navigation arrangements, though Iran's own foreign ministry acknowledged no change in actual transit conditions has resulted .

Why it matters: The Strait of Hormuz is a trade route through which some 20% of the world's oil and natural gas once passed . War-risk insurance for tankers now prices at 8.0× pre-crisis, with 6 P&I clubs withdrawing cover . Even a genuine ceasefire will take days to translate into observable AIS recovery — the transit signal remains the definitive real-world confirmation gate.

Implication: Maintain full FM clause review posture on Hormuz-origin contracts. Do not price in ceasefire relief until ≥3 consecutive days of AIS transit above 40 vessels/day are confirmed. Diplomatic signal is real but operationally ahead of physical reality.

Sources: NBC News / IMF PortWatch (Tier 1) 21 Jul; NPR (Tier 1) 26 Jul; UN IMO via UN News (Tier 1) 24 Jul; straits.live (Tier 3) 26 Jul; Windward MIOC (Tier 2) 27 Jul.

Military / Kinetic

  • Pickaxe Mountain window still technically open: President Trump said the US would target Pickaxe Mountain — "We'll be hitting that area pretty soon, and very heavily" ; Netanyahu plans to press Trump in their meeting Tuesday to keep a "constant, constant watch" on Iran's nuclear program . Strike window closes EOD 28 Jul; P now reduced to ~0.22 given diplomatic context.

Why it matters: The mutual pause is the longest operational stand-down since fighting resumed in July. Netanyahu is travelling to Washington as the United States considers whether to intensify military operations against Iran — the summit outcome on 29 Jul is the primary determinant of whether the pause converts to framework or collapses. The Houthi axis, however, is not subject to any US–Iran bilateral agreement and continues independent kinetic escalation of Saudi energy infrastructure.

Implication: P(CENTCOM resumption by EOD 27 Jul) = 0.35 — diplomacy suppresses resumption but Netanyahu summit may produce new strike authorisation. P(Pickaxe Mountain by EOD 28 Jul) = 0.22 ↓18pp. P(Yanbu sustained damage by EOD 30 Jul) = 0.52 ↑14pp — Houthi vow of "escalation with escalation" is the primary near-term kinetic risk.

Diplomatic / Political

Severe ↑ Confidence: Medium
  • Mutual pause + ceasefire back-channel active: A US official said both countries want to return to the interim ceasefire deal; a compromise being negotiated centres on having Iran run vessel transit through the Strait of Hormuz with fewer restrictions on ships .
  • Netanyahu–Trump summit 29 Jul — first war-era meeting: Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu will travel to Washington on Monday and will meet with US President Donald Trump on Tuesday . The visit is expected to focus on possible military action against Iran, regional security and the future of Middle East diplomacy .
  • Netanyahu nuclear pressure signal: Netanyahu said he plans to press Trump in their meeting Tuesday to keep a "constant, constant watch" on Iran's nuclear program, even as he said he has no new evidence that Tehran's ability to build a weapon has been destroyed . Counter-signal against de-escalation: the meeting comes amid tensions between Jerusalem and Washington over the Iran war; sources told Haaretz that the US seeks to pressure Israel over Lebanon, with VP JD Vance accusing Israel of promoting a 'perpetual war' campaign .

Why it matters: No official text of the 14-point memorandum of understanding signed in June 2026 has been published , and the interim ceasefire has been largely undermined by resumed fighting. The summit is the first direct Trump–Netanyahu strategic alignment meeting since the war began — its outcome will define whether the US doubles down on military pressure or endorses a structured de-escalation. If Netanyahu makes the trip, it could suggest major US escalation against Iran is on hold .

Implication: P(new diplomatic framework text by EOD 31 Jul) = 0.18 ↑6pp — highest in tracker history. P(CENTCOM resumption within 48h of summit) = 0.40 if summit produces no framework agreement; 0.15 if Trump endorses ceasefire return. This is the decisive 72-hour window.

04

Infrastructure / Energy

Crisis → Confidence: High
  • Brent $95 floor broken: Brent fell to $90.95 USD/bbl on July 26, 2026, down 7.56% from the previous day . Diplomatic signal drove the compression; structural disruptions remain unchanged.
  • Jizan Aramco refinery struck and ablaze: Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi movement struck Saudi Aramco's refinery complex in Jizan before dawn Saturday, setting it on fire, and launched a simultaneous attack on the Yanbu export terminal . Kpler data confirmed Yanbu accounted for 92% of Saudi Arabia's seaborne crude exports in June 2026 , at historic throughput highs.
  • CPC disruption ongoing: The Caspian Pipeline Consortium suspended crude loadings at its Black Sea terminal after tanker attacks, disrupting around 80% of Kazakhstan's oil exports .

Why it matters: Yanbu is Saudi Arabia's entire export operation during the Hormuz closure — there is no third corridor. Kuwait, Qatar, and Iraq have no comparable overland alternatives; their Hormuz-dependent exports are completely stopped . The Jizan fire and Yanbu intercept represent the most serious direct threat to the world's most important currently-functioning energy export corridor. The EIA's July 2026 Short-Term Energy Outlook forecast global oil consumption would decrease by an average of 1.2 million barrels per day in 2026 , creating a partial offset to supply suppression at these price levels.

Implication: Brent may test $85–88 range on sustained diplomatic progress; Yanbu physical damage would spike to $100+. FM clause review on Saudi-origin long-tenor contracts is now unambiguously triggered regardless of Hormuz status. Price direction over next 72h is asymmetric to summit outcome and Yanbu physical status.

Sources: TradingEconomics (Tier 1) 26 Jul; Al Jazeera (Tier 1) 26 Jul; CNBC (Tier 1) 25 Jul; Reuters (Tier 1) 25 Jul; Investing.com / EIA (Tier 1) Jul 2026; Signal Ocean / AFP (Tier 2) cited in TechTimes analysis.
05

Alternative Routes

Crisis ↓ Confidence: High
  • Bab al-Mandeb at new low: Ship traffic through Bab el-Mandeb fell on Sunday after Yemeni Houthis attacked Saudi oil installations along the Red Sea coast; eleven commodity vessels passed through the Bab el-Mandeb strait on Sunday, the lowest level in months .
  • Saudi counter-strikes on Houthis: Saudi Arabia said its coalition forces struck Houthi militia in Yemen after the Iran-backed group claimed to have attacked two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea . Houthi spokesman vowed the group would respond to "escalation with escalation" .
  • Asian buyers pivoting to Cape: Asian buyers have begun discussing rerouting Saudi crude shipments through the Suez Canal and around Africa . Cape of Good Hope remains sole functioning large-volume alternative.

Why it matters: Red Sea traffic has been disrupted off the coast of Yemen since last week by the Tehran-aligned Houthis, who want to blockade Saudi exports, expanding the US-Iran conflict that has already choked oil supply through the Strait of Hormuz . The Houthi axis is architecturally independent of the US–Iran bilateral — a US–Iran ceasefire does not automatically end Houthi enforcement of the Saudi blockade. Saudi Arabia pushed its East-West Pipeline to an all-time throughput record of 7 million barrels per day in March 2026 , making Yanbu irreplaceable; its suppression severs the last meaningful alternative to Hormuz for Gulf crude.

Implication: Extend Cape buffer to 90-day minimum for any supply chain carrying Gulf-origin or Yanbu-routed crude. Do not reduce buffer on Hormuz diplomatic signal alone — Bab al-Mandeb/Yanbu axis is independently escalating. P(Yanbu sustained physical damage by EOD 30 Jul) = 0.52.

Sources: Reuters/Kpler (Tier 1) 27 Jul; CNBC (Tier 1) 25 Jul; Al Jazeera (Tier 1) 26 Jul; TradingEconomics (Tier 1) 26 Jul; The National (Tier 2) 26 Jul; gCaptain (Tier 2) via straits.live.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
