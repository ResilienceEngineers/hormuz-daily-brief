Diplomatic tempo sharply accelerated on 4–5 August: US Treasury Secretary Bessent told CNBC a Hormuz deal could arrive "today or tomorrow," while Secretary of State Rubio confirmed "progress but not finality" on Iran–Oman route talks, sending Brent crude below $80 for the first time since 13 July. However, no public deal text has been released as of 06:01 UTC 5 August, Iran continues to insist it is negotiating with Oman only (not the US directly), and the IRGC's Persian Gulf Strait Authority toll regime — per-vessel fees up to $2 million in yuan, Bitcoin, or USDT — remains fully operational. Abqaiq has now been silent for 12 days (7 M bpd of processing capacity offline with zero Aramco public statement), and CENTCOM confirmed the Oman-corridor southern route is "free and open" while acknowledging interceptor stockpiles are severely depleted — a structural brake on any US resumption of strikes. The modal scenario (B: frozen attrition / diplomatic limbo) holds at approximately 50% as Bessent optimism has not yet produced a verifiable operational change.

01

Maritime / Hormuz

Crisis → Confidence: High
  • Kpler/CNN (T1, 4 Aug): 9 confirmed transits Sunday; 7 via Iranian route; 2 tankers; transponder evasion complicating count; crude flows ~3–5 M bpd (Marisks)
  • GlobalSecurity/Lloyd's List (T1/T2): "No Western-allied transits since 4 May"; IRGC Persian Gulf Strait Authority toll regime documented — up to $2 M/vessel payable in yuan, Bitcoin, or USDT
  • CENTCOM.mil (Hard T1, 4 Aug): Southern route "free and open"; assisted 1,000+ vessels since conflict began — but AIS shows transits still ~7% of 130/day pre-crisis norm

Why it matters: The gap between CENTCOM's "open" declaration and Kpler's 9-transit reality illustrates the core information hazard: physical flows remain 93% below baseline regardless of diplomatic framing. The ~3–5 M bpd flow through Hormuz versus 20 M bpd pre-crisis means two-thirds of Gulf crude is still rerouted or offline, sustained by SPR drawdowns and non-Gulf supply that are not unlimited.

Implication: Continue treating Hormuz as operationally closed for planning purposes. A verified deal text would not immediately restore transit volume — mine clearance, IRGC stand-down, and insurer re-entry each require additional 30–60 days. FM triggers remain live.

Sources: Kpler via CNN (T1) 4 Aug; Marisks/CNN (T1) 4 Aug; GlobalSecurity (T2) 4 Aug; CENTCOM.mil (Hard T1) 4–5 Aug; Lloyd's List Intelligence (T1) via GlobalSecurity.

If Bessent deadline (EOD 6 Aug) passes without deal text, P(resumption within 48h of missed deadline) rises to 0.40–0.45, partially offset by interceptor constraint.

Infrastructure / Energy

Crisis → Confidence: High
  • Yahoo Finance/OilPrice/PeakOil (T1/T2, 2–4 Aug): Abqaiq Day 12 — Aramco "remarkably quiet"; no restart timeline; no throughput statement; facility processes 7 M bpd (5–7% of global supply); silence is material information hazard by Day 7+ heuristic
  • Caliber.Az/NASA FIRMS (Hard T1, 27 Jul): Satellite imagery confirms extensive fire activity at Abqaiq; East-West Pumping Station also struck; emergency flaring at multiple sites; Saudi Arabia blamed Iran-backed Iraqi resistance groups
  • IndexBox/AFP (T2, 25 Jul): Jazan refinery 400 k bpd offline through mid-August; IGCC + tank farm damaged; Houthi claim confirmed via AFP satellite video; Petroline East-West (7 M bpd) in emergency NGL conversion mode

Why it matters: Three Saudi export nodes simultaneously compromised. Abqaiq processes the bulk of Saudi crude (Ghawar, Khurais, Shaybah fields) before export — it is not replaceable by any other facility. The Petroline offset covers crude volume only; refined products (Abqaiq stabilisation, Jazan refining) are not recoverable through alternative routes at equivalent scale. Day 12 Aramco silence exceeds the historical pattern for precautionary shutdowns, raising P(significant structural damage) above P(precautionary pause).

Implication: FM is mandatory at execution level for all energy commodity contracts. Aramco statement is a binary price event: confirmation of significant impairment = Brent $98–110 immediate. Diplomatic pause reducing Saudi cover for continued silence — P(statement by EOD 7 Aug) = 0.65 (unchanged from Day 157).

Sources: Yahoo Finance (T1) 31 Jul–4 Aug; OilPrice/PeakOil (T2) 2–4 Aug; NASA FIRMS (Hard T1) 27 Jul; Caliber.Az (T2) 27 Jul; IndexBox (T2) 25–27 Jul; AFP satellite imagery (Hard T1) 25 Jul.

The Cape of Good Hope stress index at 91/100 (extreme) signals the alternative route buffer is near exhaustion. Saudi tanker Cape-diversions confirm the blockade is operationally real despite HOCC denial.

Implication: Lock 150-day VLCC Cape buffer minimum. If Houthi fee implementation formalises, dual-chokepoint toll regime becomes the new normal for non-Chinese shipping, requiring structural rerouting and freight rate renegotiation for all long-tenor contracts.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
