Maritime & chokepoints

Severe → Confidence: High
  • UKMTO #212 (18 Nov, time-late 19 Nov): tanker struck by unknown projectile 6NM N of Khasab, engine-room hit, crew safe — 6th UKMTO attack-class warning since 10 Nov (Tier 1 Hard).
  • hormuz.now (20 Nov): 5 AIS-visible transits 19 Nov; Nov 2026 avg 6.9/day; UANI 18 Nov: US-facilitated avg 27.7/day (83 transits 12–14 Nov) — down from 33.8/day 8–11 Nov (Tier 2).
  • Bab al-Mandeb: IMF PortWatch/hormuz.now 8.3 AIS tanker transits/day since Aug 2026; Houthis hold Dhubab–Bab al-Mandeb after Lahij coast withdrawal (AFP 16 Nov); JMIC Advisory 112: threat level SUBSTANTIAL (Tier 1).

Why it matters: The Hormuz attack cadence is structural — near-daily UKMTO warnings now extend 6 warnings in 9 days. The gap between AIS-visible (~7/day) and US-facilitated (~28/day) transits reflects the growing dependence on US naval escort, which is not scalable indefinitely and creates a single-point-of-failure risk if CENTCOM posture shifts. Bab al-Mandeb throughput is now less than half its pre-Houthi-advance level.

Supply-chain implication: War-risk insurance clauses are active across the Gulf VRA. Cargo owners on vessels transiting Hormuz without US escort face near-daily attack probability. Red Sea rerouting is constrained by Houthi coast consolidation; Cape routing absorbs overflow at +$2–3M/voyage premium. Spot tanker rates remain elevated structurally.

Alternative routes

Concerning → Confidence: Medium
  • Cape of Good Hope: ~15.5 AIS tanker transits/day (hormuz.now Nov 2026) — absorbing Suez-rerouted cargo; structural capacity approaching ceiling for certain vessel classes (Tier 2).
  • Bab al-Mandeb: 8.3 AIS tanker transits/day (down from 14–15/day Mar–Jun 2026); Houthi coast consolidation along Lahij removed Yemeni gov forces from Dhubab airport and Bab al-Mandeb approaches (AFP 16 Nov — Tier 2).
  • Suez Canal: throughput constrained by reduced Red Sea feeder traffic; Iranian-flagged container BEHTA (IMO 9349590) transited northbound (UANI 5 Nov) — signals Russian/Iranian circumvention routes remain active (Tier 2).

Why it matters: The Cape route is absorbing unprecedented rerouted volume but at significant cost and lead-time penalty (+14 days vs Suez). Houthi Red Sea coast consolidation reduces the possibility of Bab al-Mandeb reopening without military action to clear the coast. The East–West pipeline (Saudi) remains structurally intact but Houthi targeting pattern escalation (W5) threatens the route's insurance viability.

Supply-chain implication: Cape routing should be treated as the structural baseline through at least Q1 2027. Any cargo currently routed via Red Sea/Suez faces materially elevated war-risk exposure. Consider contracted buffer stock at European receiving terminals to absorb lead-time variance.

Sources: hormuz.now Nov 2026 (Tier 2) · AFP/Japan Times 16 Nov (Tier 2) · UANI 5 Nov (Tier 2)

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
