Threat level
4 / 5 · Severe
Iran's army and IRGC executed a second confirmed retaliatory wave on 2–3 September, striking Al Minhad Air Base (UAE), Ahmed Al-Jaber Air Base (Kuwait), and targets in Jordan and Iraq — the most sustained multi-day exchange since the war's opening week. No confirmed US fatalities from the Sep 2–3 strikes have been reported; most incoming missiles and drones were intercepted by Bahrain, Jordan and Kuwait air defences, but damage to communications systems and aircraft hangars at Kuwait was claimed by Iran and undenied by the US. Brent crude hit an intraday high of $99.38 on 3 September before settling near $95–96, a gain of more than 7% on the week; tanker crossings at Hormuz collapsed to four vessels on 2 September (Kpler), against a wartime peak of 17 million barrels on 1 September — illustrating the extreme daily volatility in actual transit flow. Trump's comment that hostilities would not last "much longer" is assessed as Soft-tier rhetoric rather than a structural de-escalation signal, as no diplomatic channel, ceasefire term, or OFAC waiver has been announced.

Maritime / Hormuz

Crisis ↑ Confidence: High
  • Hormuz transits collapsed to 4 tanker crossings 2 Sep (Kpler T2); 8 vessels 3 Sep window (Windward T2); against baseline 85/day. Wartime peak 17 Mb on 1 Sep immediately reversed.
  • CENTCOM struck approximately 100 IRGC-linked targets 1 Sep including air-defence, radar, maritime assets, mine-laying, comms (CENTCOM Hard T1). Windward (T2) reports media claims of two Iranian government tankers struck 2 Sep — not independently AIS/imagery confirmed.
  • UKMTO 122-26 and 124-26 in force; MARAD-006, -004, -008 active. 408+ vessels holding position (Straits.live T3). AIS-dark transits excluded — true flow may be marginally higher.

Why it matters: The 1 Sep wartime-record 17 Mb single-day transit followed by a collapse to 4 vessels the next day illustrates the extreme binary nature of Hormuz flow — a brief US-escorted surge does not represent structural reopening. The corridor is effectively closed to commercial shipping; individual escort-passage events are tactical, not structural.

Implication: Maintain Cape unconditionally. Any cargo booked on Hormuz transit requires war-risk underwriter confirmation and OFAC PGSA compliance check. Next hard re-entry gate: all four conditions must be met simultaneously — none are currently met.

Crisis → Confidence: High

  • Bab al-Mandeb / Red Sea: MARAD-006 in force to 22 Sep; Houthi no new confirmed commercial attack 2–4 Sep (Security Council Report T1, Jun report: zero incidents in 2026 through June); MV Seamull + 5 vessels held; IRGC activation pressure on Houthis ongoing. Capability intact.
  • Iran–Oman corridor: OFAC PGSA blocks compliance; attacked twice (Sidr, Senegal Prosperity 31 Aug/1 Sep); no commercial transits. Corridor revenue-share agreement moot pending PGSA waiver.

No combination of alternatives can substitute Hormuz throughput at scale.

Implication: Cape unconditional. For BAM: any Houthi activation signal (IRGC direct instruction confirmed, or Houthi public claim on commercial vessel) would trigger immediate Cape-only mandate. Monitor W5 (MV Seamull) as early indicator of Houthi posture shift.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
