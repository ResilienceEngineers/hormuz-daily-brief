Day 271 is defined by a significant intelligence correction and a deepening dual-chokepoint pressure: Iranian state broadcaster IRIB and IranIntl (25 Nov) confirmed the widely-reported tanker convoy to China was a propaganda operation — TankerTrackers debunked both named vessels, IRNA stated "no convoy was moving," and satellite imagery of Kharg showed no significant loading. The blockade enforcement cadence continues unchanged: UKMTO Warning 214-26 (20 Nov tanker struck 8NM north of Khasab) remains the latest confirmed kinetic event; CENTCOM attributed the 7 Nov M/V Huda disabling but has stayed silent on the 22 Nov Chabahar "military forces" incident. On the Yemen axis, AP and Reuters (24 Nov) confirmed Houthi forces have advanced to within ~60 km northwest of Aden; Yemeni government forces report a fortified defensive line north and west of the city at Tur Al-Bahah. The critical OPEC+ 29 November ministerial meeting — which will set the 2027 output framework — arrives with no authorized production baseline beyond 31 December 2026, adding structural price uncertainty to an already supply-disrupted market; Brent settled at $87.46 on 25 Nov (−22% month-to-date), reflecting supply-glut read more than war premium.

Six categories

01

Maritime Security — Strait of Hormuz

Severe ↑ Confidence: High (UKMTO Tier 1)
  • UKMTO Warning 214-26 (20 Nov): tanker struck by unknown projectile 8NM north of Khasab, Oman — fire in engine room, no casualties; latest confirmed kinetic event in Strait.
  • UKMTO weekly (through 18 Nov): 6 verified attacks in latest reporting period (4 in Strait, 2 wider Gulf); ~19 attacks through 18 Nov — highest monthly rate since war began per UKMTO/ABC News.
  • 22 Nov Chabahar UKMTO warning: "tanker involved in incident with military forces" near Chabahar — vessel identity, forces involved and circumstances unconfirmed; CENTCOM silent 72h+.

Why it matters: The enforcement cadence at ~6 verified attacks/week represents a structural ceiling on commercial transit risk. The unattributed Chabahar incident signals CENTCOM enforcement east of the blockade line, potentially pushing Iranian countermeasures further into Gulf of Oman. Iran's convoy narrative debunked (25 Nov) reduces one specific escalation pathway but does not alter the IRGC's ongoing attack posture against outbound commercial traffic.

Supply chain implication: Hormuz transits remain ~65% below pre-war baseline. War-risk insurance premiums holding at elevated levels. Any vessel operator transiting outbound faces IRGC projectile/drone risk; inbound transits face CENTCOM enforcement/interdiction risk. Cargo routing decisions must account for 10–14 day Cape diversion cost against war-risk premium.

Sources: UKMTO Warning 214-26 (20 Nov 2026); UKMTO weekly maritime security overview (w/e 18 Nov 2026); Euronews (22 Nov 2026); BEinsure (22 Nov 2026); gCaptain (20 Nov 2026); ABC News (22 Nov 2026).
02

Military / CENTCOM Enforcement

Elevated → Confidence: Medium (CENTCOM Tier 1 for prior actions; Chabahar 22 Nov unattributed)
  • CENTCOM confirmed (7 Nov): US forces disabled M/V Huda (St. Kitts-flagged) in Gulf of Oman after vessel visited Iranian port and ignored repeated warnings; precision munitions fired at stern. CENTCOM: 147 vessels redirected, 5 disabled since Jul 2026.
  • CENTCOM advisory (20 Nov): STS (ship-to-ship) transfers in US-designated enforcement zone now prohibited — expanding blockade scope to close Iran's principal oil-evasion route.
  • Chabahar 22 Nov: UKMTO confirmed "military forces" incident; CENTCOM has not publicly attributed; Iran's IRNA reported explosions on tankers near Chabahar but noted no confirmed connection to 22 Nov incident. Attribution silence now 72h+.

Why it matters: CENTCOM's expanding enforcement posture (STS ban + Chabahar-proximity operations) signals a tightening of the economic blockade. The 72h+ Chabahar silence is consistent with the CENTCOM 48–96h attribution lag pattern observed in prior enforcement actions. If CENTCOM attributes, Iran's retaliation clock activates — a Threat-5 candidate event chain.

Supply chain implication: Any vessel that has called at an Iranian port faces interdiction risk on outbound transit. The STS ban eliminates the last significant sanctions-evasion route in the Persian Gulf. Operators with Iranian cargo exposure face seizure/disabling risk; non-Iranian cargo operators face IRGC retaliatory targeting risk as collateral enforcement action.

Sources: CENTCOM public statement (7 Nov 2026) via BEinsure; CENTCOM STS advisory (20 Nov 2026) via Day 269 brief; UKMTO warning (22 Nov 2026); Euronews (22 Nov 2026).
03

Diplomatic — US-Iran Negotiations

Stalled → Confidence: Medium (Qatar FM Tier 2; Iran FM Tier 5 as state position)
  • Qatar FM Al-Ansari (24 Nov press briefing, Kiprinform/Gulf News): Qatar is party to US-Iran negotiations; Hormuz navigation restoration is explicitly on the negotiating agenda; Qatar will disclose details only after final accord. Qatar has stepped up diplomatic contacts with Washington and Tehran to head off renewed military escalation.
  • Iran Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf (Gulf News live blog, 25 Nov): Tehran will not engage in negotiations until seven conditions met — blockade lifting, sanctions repeal, frozen fund release, end to all hostilities, formal war-end declaration. Iran summoned Qatari envoy over Qatar-Germany joint statement condemning attacks on shipping and urging Hormuz freedom of navigation.
  • Trump (AP, via CBS/Al Jazeera Oct–Nov): warned of new strikes after year-end if no agreement; accused Tehran of not being serious. No new US concession signals in 24h window.

Why it matters: Qatar explicitly confirming Hormuz restoration is on the negotiating table is a marginal positive signal — slightly widening the Scenario A corridor from 8% to 10%. However, Iran's public seven-conditions reiteration on the same day signals the gap between Iranian pre-conditions and US positions remains wide. The Iran–Qatar friction over the joint statement with Germany indicates Iran is using diplomatic pressure to constrain mediator scope.

Supply chain implication: No diplomatic breakthrough within 30d planning horizon. Continue treating Hormuz closure as structural baseline. Insurance and routing decisions should not price in diplomatic relief until at minimum: (a) Iran publicly signals conditionality shift, and (b) CENTCOM pauses enforcement operations pending talks.

Sources: Qatar FM briefing via Kiprinform (24 Nov 2026); Gulf News live blog (25 Nov 2026); Al Jazeera (2 Nov 2026); CBS News live updates (2 Nov 2026).
04

Yemen / Houthi — Dual Chokepoint

Escalating ↑ Confidence: High (AP/Reuters/Arab News Tier 2 corroborated)
  • AP and Reuters (24 Nov): Houthi forces advanced to within ~60 km (40 miles) NW of Aden, reduced from ~100 km six days prior (18 Nov, The National). Yemeni army spokesman Al-Nuzaily confirmed defensive line established at Tur Al-Bahah, north and west of Aden, with forces redeployed from Bab al-Mandeb coast (15 Nov).
  • New Arab (22 Nov): Houthis reinforcing Al-Bayda–Abyan eastern encirclement axis; Southern Armed Forces stepped up defensive measures; analysts assess Abyan control would enable Houthi encirclement of Aden from east and isolation from eastern governorates.
  • houseofsaud.com (25 Nov): Houthi missile strikes on Riyadh ongoing; PIF Tower struck again 25 Nov — dual strategy of ground advance on Aden plus nightly Saudi capital strikes.

Why it matters: The 40 km / 10-day advance rate is the most significant ground development since Houthis consolidated Red Sea coast control in September 2026. If Tur Al-Bahah defensive line is breached, Houthis approach the dual-chokepoint threshold: simultaneous threat to Bab al-Mandeb maritime passage (Perim Island already held) and Aden port. Saudi defensive posture is strained by simultaneous Riyadh missile strikes. This is the primary Scenario C accelerant.

Supply chain implication: Bab al-Mandeb carriers resuming cautious transits (Suez recovery) must price in rapidly evolving southern Yemen ground situation. If Aden falls or Houthis breach the defensive line, the Red Sea partial recovery reverses. Southbound Saudi crude transits already off-limits (Houthi naval ban since 20 Jul 2026). Ethiopian and East African trade facing compounding risk from both Bab al-Mandeb instability and Djibouti port disruption.

Sources: AP (24 Nov 2026); Reuters (24 Nov 2026); Arab News (24 Nov 2026); New Arab (22 Nov 2026); houseofsaud.com (25 Nov 2026); Horn Review (19 Nov 2026).
05

Energy Markets / OPEC+

Watch ↓ Confidence: High (OPEC communiqué Tier 1; Brent data Tier 1)
  • Brent crude: $87.46 close (25 Nov, Investing.com); down −1.49% on day; −22% month-to-date. Libya Sharara pipeline restart and supply-glut narrative suppressing war premium. Brent was at $90.64 on 24 Nov (Armenpress), indicating continued downward pressure.
  • OPEC+ 29 Nov ministerial (42nd full OPEC+ meeting + 69th JMMC): First meeting to set 2027 output framework; no authorized production baseline exists beyond 31 Dec 2026. Capacity audit results land at this meeting. OPEC communiqué (1 Nov): December 2026 targets unchanged at September 2026 levels; next meeting 29 Nov.
  • OPEC+ structural context (Reuters Oct 2026): Gulf producers pumping well below output targets due to continuing export disruptions from Iran war; output capacity review delayed by uncertainty thrown up by conflict. UAE has exited OPEC (May 2026); 21-member group convenes 29 Nov.

Why it matters: The 29 Nov meeting is a binary price-structure event: an output increase decision (even on paper) would push Brent further below $85, compressing Iran's war-chest replenishment calculus and potentially weakening Iranian resistance to blockade. Conversely, another deferral — plausible given UAE exit, capacity-review complexity, and member under-production — would be read as OPEC+ unable to coalesce, adding to supply uncertainty.

Supply chain implication: Refined product and long-tenor contract holders should model two paths: (a) OPEC+ output increase → Brent $82–85 by Jan 2027, compressing refinery margins but reducing Iran's oil-export desperation; (b) no framework agreed → Brent bounces on supply uncertainty. Neither resolves the Hormuz structural disruption; oil pricing and shipping costs remain decoupled.

Sources: Investing.com Brent futures history (25 Nov 2026); OPEC official communiqué (1 Nov 2026); Reuters via StreetInsider (Oct 2026); Admiral Markets OPEC+ preview (20 Nov 2026); houseofsaud.com OPEC+ analysis (23 Nov 2026); Armenpress (25 Nov 2026).

Outlook — Scenario Assessment

  • Scenario A (Negotiated framework) corridor marginally wider: Qatar FM confirming Hormuz on agenda (24 Nov) is a qualitative positive, but Iran's public seven-conditions reiteration (25 Nov) and summoning of Qatari envoy signal no near-term Iranian movement. P: 10% (↑+2pp from Day 270).
  • Scenario B (Frozen attrition) corridor widened: tanker convoy debunked (25 Nov) removes the most acute near-term kinetic accelerant; Suez partial recovery creates a functioning alt-route buffer. CENTCOM enforcement continues at steady state. P: 25% (↑+3pp from Day 270).
  • Scenario C (Re-escalation) remains modal but probability reduced: Houthi 60-km Aden advance, 31 laden tankers near blockade line, Chabahar unattributed incident, Iran seven-conditions deadlock, and nightly Saudi missile strikes maintain structural re-escalation pressure. P: 65% (↓−5pp from Day 270).

Why it matters: The convoy debunking is a significant intelligence correction that reduces one specific near-term escalation pathway. It does not alter the structural conditions: Iran's economic desperation, CENTCOM tightening enforcement, Houthis closing on Aden, and no diplomatic breakthrough. The probability mass shifts modestly from C to B, not to A.

Supply chain implication: Modal scenario (65%) remains re-escalation within 30 days. Supply chain continuity plans should maintain Cape routing as primary for Hormuz-dependent cargo; treat Suez partial recovery as an opportunity for non-tanker cargo while monitoring Houthi–Aden frontline closely. The 29 Nov OPEC+ meeting is the next scheduled binary event.

Sources: Day 271 intelligence synthesis across UKMTO, CENTCOM, Qatar FM, IranIntl, AP/Reuters, Gulf News, Investing.com, OPEC secretariat.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
