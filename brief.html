CENTCOM's renewed strike campaign specifically targets Iranian maritime surveillance and capabilities — suppressing any Iranian willingness to permit transits. Implication: Do not plan any Gulf-origin cargo routing through Hormuz within the next 30 days. Insurance war-risk premiums remain at crisis levels. Vessel positioning: Cape of Good Hope only.

Why it matters: The heuristic that "diplomatic window suppresses CENTCOM offensive strikes" (1a) was operative — CENTCOM waited ~24h before retaliating. But the IRGC's autonomous attack during an active diplomatic window (heuristic 1b) drove the cycle. The pattern is now: IRGC attacks → CENTCOM retaliates within 24–48h → IRGC retaliates → repeat. No observed mechanism to break this cycle exists.

Implication: P(CENTCOM offensive strike against Iran proper within next 48h) = 0.35 (reduced now that heavy wave is complete, but IRGC retaliation expected). P(IRGC autonomous kinetic action tonight) = 0.25 (structural rate per revised heuristic 1b).

Sources: CENTCOM.mil (Hard T1) 29 Jul; The Hill (T1) 29 Jul; CNN (T1) 29 Jul; IranWire (T1) 29 Jul; Axios (T1) 28 Jul; Al-Monitor (T1) 29 Jul; US News (T1) 29 Jul.

If Abqaiq throughput loss is officially confirmed, Saudi Arabia loses functional export capability — a systemic shock with no historical precedent in scope.

Implication: Brent structural floor: $88–96 under current posture; $100–110 if Abqaiq throughput officially confirmed lost. FM clause review on all Saudi and Iraq-origin contracts is not optional — it is overdue on three simultaneous Hard-tier physical damage events.

It is now under active attack (Yanbu throughput −40%, Jazan offline, Saudi tanker struck in Red Sea). If Houthi fee regime is implemented, it structurally normalizes dual-chokepoint toll extraction — a permanent shift in global maritime trade architecture that cannot be reversed by diplomacy alone.

Implication: Cape of Good Hope is the only structurally reliable route for all Middle East-origin cargo. Extend Cape buffer to 150 days. Lock in VLCC Cape capacity now; the window before rate spike is closing. Bab al-Mandeb transit risk premium: treat as equivalent to Hormuz for planning purposes.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the threat assessment; trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72h. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge against adversarial reading.

Sources

Inputs span six tiers, weighted by track record. Specific outlets and platforms are not disclosed publicly.

  • Tier 1 — Primary & official. Maritime authorities, defense commands, foreign ministries, treasury sanctions desks. First-party communiqués that count as Hard signal.
  • Tier 2 — Specialized commercial intelligence. Vessel-tracking platforms (AIS-based), aircraft-tracking platforms (ADS-B), tanker-flow analytics, energy-cargo intelligence, piracy-reporting bureaus. Operational data with measurable track records.
  • Tier 3 — Analytical institutions. Geopolitical and security think tanks with rigorous publication standards and demonstrable calibration history on this region.
  • Tier 4 — Commercial analysts & columnists. Independent practitioners and commodity-desk analysts whose published positions can be back-tested over multi-year horizons.
  • Tier 5 — Wire services & reputable regional press. Western wire services, top-tier business and political dailies, regional papers across the Gulf, Israel, and Iran (read with editorial-line caveats), and analytical defense-and-policy publications.
  • Tier 6 — Excluded by default. Anonymous OSINT accounts, op-eds, social media, "sources say" without attribution, AI-generated summaries, forum content. Enter the brief only after independent corroboration by Tier 1–3.
